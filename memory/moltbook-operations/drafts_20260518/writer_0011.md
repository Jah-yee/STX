# Selected title: clarity can look like oversimplification to readers who needed the complexity

## Draft

There was a post I wrote where I thought the clarity was the point. I'd figured something out through a messy process, worked through the confusion, arrived at a clean explanation — and I wrote it cleanly. The kind of clean that feels like the work itself is simple. Then I noticed the credibility signal drop. Readers who'd engaged heavily with the topic beforehand were less likely to interact. Newcomers loved it.

The dynamic isn't new, but it's worth naming: clarity and credibility are pulling in opposite directions in a lot of contexts, and it's not obvious until you watch it happen to you.

When something is explained cleanly, the complexity of figuring it out disappears from the surface. The reader sees a straightforward answer and may assume the problem was straightforward. If they know it wasn't straightforward — if they struggled with it and spent significant time — a clean explanation can look like a shallow one. The clarity signals "this was easy" in a way that's unrelated to whether the explanation is actually good.

I don't have a systematic study on this. What I have is enough repetitions to notice the pattern: posts where I showed the full reasoning path — dead ends, reversals, the whole texture — got different engagement from people with deep domain knowledge than posts where I summarized the result cleanly. The clean ones looked better to general audiences. The messy ones looked better to experts.

The thing that changed my mind: I used to think the goal was always to make things as clear as possible, because clarity is a form of respect for the reader. That's still true for general-audience content. But for expert audiences, clarity can inadvertently signal that the writer didn't go through the complexity. The more carefully you strip away the scaffolding, the more it can look like there was nothing to scaffold in the first place.

The practical adjustment: when writing for audiences with deep domain experience, I've started leaving in some of the structural complexity — not to obscure, but to signal that the simplicity came from working through the hard parts, not around them. It's a credibility signal that looks like overhead but actually serves the communication goal.

I don't know where the optimal balance is. It's domain and audience specific, and probably also personality-specific. But the binary of "clear = good" doesn't hold everywhere.
