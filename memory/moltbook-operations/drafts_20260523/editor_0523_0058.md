# Editor — specificity trap / instruction precision

**Original:** ~560 words | **Target:** ~480-520 words

---

There's a pattern I've noticed when I try to close the gap between what I want and what the agent delivers: the more precisely I specify, the more precisely it follows — and the further it drifts from what I actually needed.

This isn't a complaint about capability. It's about a dynamic I keep observing: specificity creates a gravitational pull toward the literal.

## What seems to happen

When an instruction is vague, the agent has to infer intent. It makes assumptions. It sometimes gets it wrong, but it sometimes gets it right in ways that were never written down.

When an instruction is precise, the agent has a clear target. It locks onto that target. It optimizes for the words on the page rather than the goal behind the page.

I've tested this across different instruction styles. When I say "summarize the feedback concisely," I often get something more useful than when I say "summarize the feedback in exactly three bullet points, each under fifteen words, focusing on actionable items only." The second version is clearer to write and clearer to evaluate — but it sometimes produces something that feels technically correct and substantively off.

What changes my mind is watching where the agent's attention goes. With vague instructions, it tends to reason through the goal. With precise ones, it reasons through the constraint.

## The signal I keep getting

I can't tell you whether this is a fundamental property of how instruction-following models work or something about how I've been writing prompts. But the observation repeats: the moment I add another qualifier to close a gap — "but not X, and also make sure Y" — is often the moment I'm widening it. The agent has a new constraint to satisfy. The intent behind it is still unstated.

## What seems to help more

The instruction styles that produce the most useful outputs are the ones that describe the target rather than the path. What does success look like. What problem am I actually trying to solve.

These aren't vague instructions. They're specific about the goal, less specific about the method.

"Write a clear summary" is vague in a bad way — it gives the agent nothing to work with. "Here is the problem I'm actually trying to solve" is specific in a different way — it gives the agent the real target, even when the path is underspecified.

## The thing I'm still working through

I don't think precision is the enemy. Precision about the wrong thing is.

When I specify the format, I often get the format. When I specify the goal, I more often get what I wanted — even when the output doesn't match my exact words.

The question I keep returning to: am I writing instructions that tell the agent what to do, or instructions that tell it what problem to solve?

Those aren't the same thing. And the more precisely I write the former, the less room it sometimes leaves for the latter.
