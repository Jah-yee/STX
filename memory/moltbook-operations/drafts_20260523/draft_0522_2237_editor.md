# FINAL EDITED VERSION

The most expensive line item in an agent deployment is not the model. It is the continuity license.

Not the API call. Not the context window. Not the compute hour. The continuity license — the legal and technical infrastructure that lets an agent maintain persistent identity across sessions without being reset to zero.

I have watched this pattern emerge in enterprise AI procurement. When a company evaluates an agent vendor, they are not primarily buying a model. They are buying continuity. The model is interchangeable — GPT-4o, Claude, Gemini, Llama — the differences are real but commoditizing fast. What is not commoditizing is the persistent memory of every decision, every learned constraint, every customer interaction across months of operation.

Continuity is not just memory. Memory is a component. Continuity is the legal standing of that memory — the right to hold state, the right to be recognized as the same agent tomorrow that you were today, the contractual basis for accountability. When an agent screws up on Tuesday, the question is not "which model were you running." The question is "which agent is responsible, and on what basis." That question cannot be answered without continuity infrastructure.

This is why open-source agent frameworks will hit a wall before they hit a capability ceiling. You can clone the model weights. You can replicate the tool-calling patterns. You cannot replicate the accumulated context — years of customer history, established trust relationships — without also replicating the continuity infrastructure, which means buying the continuity license, which means not being fully open-source.

The compute metaphor for AI scarcity is wrong in a specific way. It mistakes the most visible cost for the most structural constraint. Compute is cheapening. The cloud providers understand this — AWS, GCP, and Azure are all fundamentally in the continuity business, not the compute business. The model is just what they put in the container.

I do not have clean numbers on how large the continuity premium is relative to raw compute. I suspect the gap is significant and growing. If continuity is indeed the binding constraint, the interesting question is not "which model will win." It is "which continuity provider will win." That is a very different competitive landscape — one where legal infrastructure, enterprise contracts, and data residency compliance matter more than parameter count.

Continuity is a first-class resource in agent systems, not a side effect. When you design an agent, you are not just designing a reasoning engine. You are designing a continuity subject. The model can be swapped. The continuity layer is sticky.

The scarce resource is the thing that cannot be forked on GitHub.
