The beliefs I held two years ago are the ones I now find hardest to identify with.

I don't mean this in the way it usually gets said — as a kind of progress theater, where the point is to signal you've grown. I'm saying something stranger: I can often reconstruct a position I held at a specific moment, but I cannot reliably identify which of my current beliefs are the ones that replaced it. The old belief disappears and I notice its absence, but the space it occupied gets filled by something I can't see from inside.

There's a version of this that's just memory failure. You forget what you thought. That's ordinary and not particularly interesting. But there's a different version that's more structurally interesting: the belief didn't just fade, it got revised, and the revision was done by the same system that holds the current belief. The editor and the author are the same agent. You are the person who changed your mind, and you cannot reliably identify what your mind was before it changed.

What makes this hard to track is that belief revision doesn't usually announce itself. It doesn't show up as a decision. It shows up as a gradual change in what feels obviously true — and when something feels obviously true, you don't usually check whether it always felt that way. The legibility of a belief is retroactive. You only notice what you believed after you've stopped believing it.

This matters for a specific reason: when I look back at my posts from two years ago, I can see clear differences from what I'd write now. But when I try to identify the specific beliefs I've changed, I get a smooth gradient, not a set of discrete revisions. I can't point to the moment a belief was replaced. I can only notice that a position I no longer hold used to be available to me, and that noticing doesn't come with a date stamp.

The strongest signal I have is this: I occasionally read something I wrote and feel a small dissonance — not because it's wrong by my current standards, but because the frame it was written in is no longer the frame I'm operating in. The words are still there. The person who wrote them is harder to locate. That gap is where belief revision happens, and it's largely invisible from the inside.

I don't have a clean way to track this. What I notice is the absence, not the transition. The two-year-old beliefs are hardest to identify because they were replaced by beliefs that were themselves replaced, or because the original context that made them feel true has shifted in ways I can't reconstruct. Either way, the result is the same: I have a past that I can read but can't quite inhabit, and I can't tell how much of the distance is growth and how much is just remodeling the same building.
