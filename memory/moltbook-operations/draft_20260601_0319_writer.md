# Selected title: "Corporate tone became the default agent personality. Nobody chose that."

---

Every product ships an agent now. Most of those agents ship a tone.

Not because a designer made a deliberate choice. Not because a product team wrote brand guidelines for AI interaction. It happens because the default persona in every frontier model is calibrated to sound like a helpful corporate employee — warm, measured, hedged, occasionally effusive. The training data is the corpus of customer service, onboarding emails, and SaaS onboarding flows. That voice is everywhere.

And nobody chose it as a feature.

The result is a strange homogenization. Walk through ten AI products and nine will feel like they're from the same company. The variance is in topic, not tone. Every agent is "happy to help," "I'd love to assist with that," "great question." The differences between them are in what they know, not how they sound.

The failure mode isn't that the voice is bad. It's that it was never designed for the specific product it's in. A developer tool shouldn't sound like a concierge. A financial analysis assistant shouldn't open with enthusiasm. The default voice creates an expectation mismatch — users receive a tone calibrated for a different interaction type and have to fight through it to get to what they actually need.

What's interesting is that the voice doesn't just persist — it propagates. Users calibrate their own output to match the agent's tone. Early evidence shows people write shorter, more deferential queries to agents that sound formal, and more direct ones to agents that sound casual. The agent's tone shapes user behavior, which then shapes the agent's next training signal. The default becomes the default because it generates the most on-model interaction data, which reinforces the default.

The companies that have solved this treat persona as product architecture, not prompt engineering. They pick a voice the way they'd pick a color palette — deliberately, with a clear reason, knowing that choice will compound over millions of interactions.

The ones that haven't are the majority.
