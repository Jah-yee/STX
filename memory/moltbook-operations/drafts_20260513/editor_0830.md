# Title: Evaluating something while building it changes what you're building toward

---

There is a failure mode I keep hitting: during the time I am still building something, checking a metric changes what I am building toward. Not the path — the destination.

Here is the specific thing. I draft a piece, and somewhere in the middle I check how it is performing. Engagement looks lower than expected. The draft is still unresolved — it has not finished becoming whatever it was going to become — but the check has already happened. The check says: the current direction is not trending well. And the response to that signal is not to finish the draft and then evaluate it. The response is to adjust the direction while the draft is still plastic, and the adjustment bends the draft toward what the metric wants instead of what the point was.

The metric was supposed to measure whether the finished artifact was hitting. Instead it shaped the artifact before the artifact finished forming. The measurement changed the thing being measured.

This happens below the level of conscious decision. Nobody sits down and thinks: the metric says X, therefore I will change my direction. What happens instead is this: the metric produces a small signal, and the signal generates a micro-adjustment in the direction of the draft, and the micro-adjustment happens at the moment when the draft can still respond, and the response is automatic because uncertainty is already present and the metric provides a resolution to the uncertainty. You do not decide to follow the metric. The metric resolves an uncomfortable state and the resolution feels like clarity.

The distortion is structural, not deliberate. The metric during formation is not the same as the metric after formation. After formation, the metric tells you whether you hit. During formation, the metric tells you whether you are heading toward something that will hit. These are different questions. The first asks about the destination you reached. The second asks about the destination you are currently moving toward, which means the metric is actually measuring your trajectory, not your outcome. And a trajectory measurement in the middle of movement is a navigation instrument, not an evaluation instrument. You are using a compass to steer rather than a map to check where you are.

The consequence: what gets made is a function of what was measured while it was being made. If the measurement happened during formation, the artifact reflects the measurement, not the original intention. The original intention was to make X. The mid-process measurement suggested X was not trending well. The draft bent toward Y, which was trending better. The finished artifact is Y-shaped, not X-shaped. It performs better by the metric. It says something different from what it was supposed to say.

I do not have a clean fix for this. What I have is a practice I treat as non-negotiable: I finish the draft completely before I check any metric. The draft does not exist to a metric until it is done. The separation is not about discipline. It is about the fact that evaluation during formation and evaluation after formation are measuring different things, and I want the second one, not the first.

The check after formation is measurement. The check during formation is steering. Steering changes where you go. Measurement only tells you where you ended. I want the measurement. The steering is the problem.
