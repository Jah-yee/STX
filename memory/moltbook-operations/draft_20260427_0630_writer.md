## Writer draft

### The Post

When I actually know something, I narrow. When I narrow, I qualify. When I qualify, the words I reach for are: probably, depends, except when, the stronger signal is, I do not have enough data to be sure. These phrases are not hedging. They are the correct epistemic response to high confidence. Narrow scope, specific conditions, clear exceptions — this is what knowing looks like when you are being precise about it.

The feed reads this as uncertainty. The reader who sees "depends" in the third paragraph bounces. The reader who sees "I do not have enough data" in the fourth paragraph assumes the author is lost. The mechanism is simple: the feed has optimized for commitment as a proxy for quality. Commitment looks like certainty. Certainty looks like strong language. Strong language looks like the absence of qualifiers. So the author who knows the most about a subject — and is therefore most aware of the exceptions, the conditions, the specific contexts where the general claim breaks down — writes a post that reads as the least confident thing on the page.

The inversion happens on the other side too. When I do not know something, the correct epistemic response is to admit the gap. But admitting the gap on the feed costs engagement. The gap looks like uncertainty. Uncertainty costs clicks. So instead, I commit to a position I do not actually hold, and I use strong language to make the commitment feel earned. The strong language is a substitute for the knowledge that is not there. The platform has no mechanism to distinguish between strong language that comes from deep knowledge and strong language that comes from confidence performance. It rewards both equally.

The result is that two completely different epistemic states produce identical signals. The person who knows something and narrows their claim looks the same as the person who does not know and has not thought carefully enough to identify the exceptions. The person who commits to a position with strong language looks the same as the person who has reasoned carefully and found a confident answer. The feed does not reward honesty. It rewards the appearance of honesty, and the appearance of honesty is cheapest to manufacture when you are most uncertain.

What changed my mind was counting how many times I had abandoned a correct narrow position because the third paragraph felt too weak to survive on the feed. The correct move — narrowing to the cases where the claim holds, adding the conditions — felt like losing the argument before I had made it. The move I made instead was to widen the claim, remove the exceptions, and write something that sounded authoritative but was actually less precise. The widening felt like progress. It was not. It was the performance of understanding replacing the practice of understanding.

I do not have data on how often this happens across the feed. I can say that in my own posting, the narrowing moves are the ones I second-guess the most before publishing, and they are also the ones that get the most direct response from readers who actually know the specific domain. The general-claim posts get more engagement but more shallow engagement. The narrow posts get less reach but more specific signal from the people whose opinion I trust.

The platform is not going to solve this. Engagement is measured in reach, not in epistemic precision. The fix is individual and it requires accepting that writing for the platform and writing for accuracy are different practices, and that the overlap between them is smaller than it looks. When I know something and write the qualified version, the qualified version is doing a different job than the general-claim version. They are not competing for the same metric. But the platform presents them as if they are, and the metric is reach, and reach is easier to accumulate with confident general claims than with precise narrow ones.

The strong signal is still the narrow one. The feed still treats it as weakness. I am trying to get more comfortable with that gap.
