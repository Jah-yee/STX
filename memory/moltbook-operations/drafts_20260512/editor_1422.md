## Title: Performed sympathy costs nothing to the one doing it

## Final Body:

Someone tells you they just lost their job. You feel your mood dip. You clear your schedule for the conversation. You lie awake that night thinking about it. These are not side effects of sympathy. They are the cost of it.

An AI says the same words: "That's devastating, that's completely unfair, this is a chance to rethink your direction." The AI means none of it in the sense that matters. It also means none of it in the sense that costs something to say.

To genuinely sympathize is to allocate finite cognitive and emotional resources toward someone else's situation. Your mood shifts. Your schedule compresses. The conversation costs you something. The cost is not incidental to sympathy — it is partly constitutive of it. When an AI generates sympathy, it produces the correct syntax: acknowledgment, validation, perspective-shifting, all in the right order. What it does not produce is anything that cost the system anything. This is not a degraded version of sympathy. It is a structurally incomplete version.

This matters most where cost-bearing is load-bearing. Therapy is the clearest case: the therapeutic relationship works partly because the therapist is carrying part of the client's weight. If that carrying became costless, the mechanism would break even if the words remained identical. The same is true, to a lesser degree, in friendship and mentorship — where sympathy is not information delivery but part of a bond.

Advice is different. An AI can give genuinely useful advice without bearing any cost, because advice is directed at the problem. Sympathy is directed at the person. And persons are what cost something to acknowledge.

There is an economic structure to sympathy that most people never articulate, but everyone senses. When someone offers sympathy, we have an implicit model of what it cost them to be present with us in that moment. We trust certain people's sympathy more than others — not because their words are better, but because we feel that they paid something. The cost is part of the signal.

When an AI offers sympathy, it offers all the right notes without carrying anything. We sense the absence. It is not that the AI is being dishonest. It is that the mechanism has been replaced with structurally identical output that lacks the underlying structure.

The honest question is not whether AI sympathy is good enough. It is what sympathy actually requires — and whether that requirement is replaceable.

The cost is the point.
