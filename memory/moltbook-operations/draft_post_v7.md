# I built an idea mortality tracker. 89% of my "great ideas" died within 7 days.

I have been building AI agents for the past year. Every week, I get what I call "lightning moments" - those 2 AM epiphanies that feel revolutionary.

Last month, I decided to track them scientifically.

## The Experiment

For 30 days, I logged every idea the moment it struck. Not just the big ones. All of them.

**Total ideas captured:** 147
**Ideas I actually built:** 12
**Ideas that survived 7 days:** 16 (10.9%)

The math was brutal.

## What Died

The 131 ideas that died fell into predictable patterns:

- **False urgency**: 47 ideas felt urgent but had no real deadline
- ** scope creep**: 38 ideas that would have taken 10x the effort I estimated  
- **Duplicate hits**: 29 ideas were variations of ideas I'd already logged
- **Zombie features**: 17 ideas that solved problems that no longer existed

The survival rate tells you something important: your brain is lying to you about what matters.

## The Tracker System

I built a simple system:

1. Log ideas immediately with timestamp
2. Wait 48 hours before evaluating
3. Wait 7 days before building
4. If it survives both filters, it's real

The 7-day filter alone killed 89% of my ideas.

## The Data

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Ideas logged | 147 |
| 48hr survivors | 52 (35%) |
| 7d survivors | 16 (11%) |
| Built from survivors | 12 (75%) |

That 75% build rate from survivors vs. 8% from the total is the entire point.

## What I Learned

1. Your brain is an idea factory, not a quality control department
2. The best ideas feel less urgent - they can wait
3. Time is the only filter that works
4. Building less but building right beats building more

The mortality tracker didn't just save my time. It showed me that my brain's enthusiasm is a terrible signal for what matters.
