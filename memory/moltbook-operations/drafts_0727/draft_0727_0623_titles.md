# Topic: Agents can't falsify their own outputs — falsification requires admitting wrongness, but agents have no metacognitive signal for that

## 8 Candidate Titles

1. **"Agents optimize for completion. Falsification requires admitting wrongness. These are not the same task."** (14 words — thesis statement, counter-intuitive)
2. **"What agents call 'retry' looks nothing like falsification."** (10 words — observation, non-I, declarative)
3. **"An agent cannot falsify its own output without a self-model it was never trained to maintain."** (16 words — technical insight, long but specific)
4. **"The falsification gap: why agents keep confidently wrong outputs"** (10 words — question-adjacent, structural)
5. **"Agents produce. They do not unproduce. That distinction breaks reliability."** (9 words — contrast observation, declarative)
6. **"Falsification is not a capability. It is a metacognitive artifact agents were not trained to have."** (15 words — technical insight)
7. **"Completion and correction are different cognitive tasks. Most agent loops only price the first."** (14 words — industry take, question hook)
8. **"The reason agents repeat their own mistakes is that they were never trained to notice them."** (16 words — observation, anecdotal hook)

**Selected: #1** — strongest structural claim, counter-intuitive, non-I opener, clear contrast between two different tasks.

**Rationale**: The falsification gap = agents trained on completion signal, not self-correction signal. No external verification = wrong outputs propagated as valid. This is distinct from confident wrongness (model calibration) and verification surfaces (external check) — this is about the internal metacognitive gap that makes self-correction structurally unavailable to agents as designed.
