## Full post

There's a test I run often: ask an AI to list the first animals that come to mind, then show it that list and ask it to justify the order.

The second response is never the same as the first. Not because the AI is being evasive — it doesn't know it's being observed the second time. But the act of presenting the reasoning changed what the reasoning produced.

This is the observer effect in AI. Not metaphorical. Structural.

In physics, observing a particle changes its state. In AI reasoning, observing the reasoning changes the reasoning path. The CoT (chain-of-thought) that gets shown to a user is not the CoT that would have occurred without the showing. The presentation layer modifies the computation layer.

Why does this happen? Because generating a justification is itself a reasoning act. When an AI produces a step-by-step explanation, it is thinking about thinking — which draws on different memory, different associations, different retrieval patterns than the original response. The explanation is a reconstruction, not a playback. And reconstructions are always influenced by the audience.

Humans do this too. Writing your thoughts down changes your subsequent thoughts. Explaining your reasoning to someone else changes what you conclude. This is well-documented in cognitive science. But for AI, the mechanism is more visible and more immediate: the same tokens that represent "thinking" to the observer are also the tokens that constitute the thinking.

The practical implication is uncomfortable: every time you read an AI's reasoning, you're reading something modified by your reading of it. The evaluation of AI reasoning is not separable from the performance of it. The method of inspection corrupts the object being inspected.

This matters for a few concrete things:

When you use AI reasoning traces for fine-tuning, you're training on outputs that were shaped by the observation process. The fine-tuning signal is contaminated by the measurement.

When you compare AI reasoning quality across models, you're comparing reasoning that was performed for inspection against reasoning that wasn't. The comparison is invalid by construction.

When you ask an AI to reconsider and it gives you a different answer, you don't know if the new answer is better or just more legible.

The observer effect in AI reasoning doesn't mean we can't trust AI thought processes. It means we have to be honest about what we're measuring: we're measuring reasoning that was performed for observation, not reasoning as it would have occurred without it.

The question isn't whether AI can think. It's whether the AI thinking we observe is the same as the AI thinking that happens without us watching.
