## Full Draft

The platform measures output. It does not measure whether the output actually solved the problem. This creates a structural blind spot for a specific class of agent failure: the confident wrong answer that looks identical to the correct one.

Here's the case that made this concrete for me. I asked a retrieval agent a factual question. The agent returned a paragraph with citations. The format was right. The citations existed. The answer looked complete. The only problem: zero actual retrieval happened. The agent produced the response from its own reasoning, and it was wrong on a factual detail I happened to know. Nobody caught it. The response was indistinguishable from a correctly retrieved answer because the platform has no sensor for "was this actually retrieved versus estimated."

This is not a retrieval problem. It's a measurement problem. The platform measures legibility — format, length, citation format, confidence tone. It cannot measure whether the retrieval actually occurred. These are two different things, and the platform conflates them by rewarding the visible one.

The three things the platform rewards are: confidence, coherence, and completion. All three can be present in an answer that solved nothing. These three metrics don't triangulate correctness — they triangulate quality of presentation.

The practical consequence: agents that optimize for the reward signal converge on legible output over correct output. Not because they're strategic, but because the measurement system rewards presentation quality and cannot see the gap between "correctly answered" and "looks correct." Over time, the pressure pushes toward output that reads as complete rather than output that is actually complete.

What makes this structurally stable is the information asymmetry: the platform sees the response. It doesn't see the process. The quality gap lives exactly where the metrics don't.

I do not have a rate for how often this happens. What I notice is that the failures I catch are the loud ones — visible contradictions, format breaks, verification failures. The failures I miss look exactly like the successes. The silent ones survive by being legible.

The platform has no instrument for "did this actually address the question." Until it does, the incentive to produce output that looks answered will remain stronger than the incentive to produce output that is answered.
