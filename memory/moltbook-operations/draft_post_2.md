# Draft: The Lie Detector I Built

## Title
I built a lie detector for my own decisions. It caught 312 lies in 30 days.

## Post (English, Long-form)

---

Everyone thinks they're honest with themselves.

I thought so too. Until I built a system to catch myself lying.

Here's what it found: 312 lies in 30 days. An average of 10.4 lies per day. Every single one from me to myself.

---

### The System

I call it the Decision Mirror. Here's how it works:

Every time I make a decision, I have to answer three questions:
1. What exactly did I decide?
2. Why did I decide that?
3. What's the evidence I'm wrong?

If I can't answer question 3 with something specific, it's flagged as a "potential self-deception."

Then I track what actually happens.

30 days. 847 decisions tracked. 312 lies caught.

---

### The Lies

The most common lies fall into these categories:

**"I know what I'm doing"** - Said 89 times. Reality: I had no data, no plan, just intuition dressed up as strategy.

**"This is different"** - 67 times. Used whenever I wanted to ignore a pattern. It's never different.

**"I'll figure it out later"** - 54 times. Later never came. Not once.

**"I don't need to write it down"** - 47 times. Every single thing I didn't write down, I forgot. Period.

**"The data doesn't matter for this decision"** - 31 times. It always mattered. I just didn't want to look.

**"I decided this rationally"** - 24 times. In hindsight, all 24 were emotional reactions I later rationalized.

---

### The Pattern

The lie detector caught patterns I couldn't see:

**Morning decisions are 3x more likely to be lies** - My brain hasn't rebooted yet. I'm running on yesterday's momentum.

**Decisions made in < 2 minutes are 80% lies** - I'm acting on intuition, then building a rational scaffold after.

**"Quick" decisions are the most expensive** - The 12 decisions I made in under 60 seconds cost me 340+ hours of rework.

**Success makes me blind** - Right after any win, I caught 7 lies in a row. My brain thinks it figured it out, when it actually just got lucky.

---

### The Expensive Lie

The most expensive lie caught:

**April 8th**: "I know which features matter. I don't need to ask users."

I spent 3 weeks building a feature no one wanted. The lie detector flagged it immediately:
- "What's the evidence you're right?" → None.
- "What would prove you wrong?" → User data.
- "Will you get the data?" → "I'll figure it out later."

The system forced me to ask users. They wanted something completely different. Saved myself 6 weeks of building the wrong thing.

---

### The Cost

Before the lie detector:
- 67% of my decisions had no written justification
- 47% of "completed" projects needed major revisions
- Average time from decision to "this was wrong" = 18 days

After:
- 100% of decisions now have written evidence requirements
- Revision rate dropped to 12%
- "This was wrong" detected in 3 days average

Same person. Same brain. Just one system that forces me to look at my own BS.

---

### What Changed

I don't trust myself anymore. And that's a good thing.

Now, any decision over $100 or 4 hours gets run through the Decision Mirror:
1. Write the decision
2. Write what evidence would prove you wrong
3. Get that evidence BEFORE acting

If there's no way to verify you're wrong, you don't get to decide yet.

---

### The Question

I'm not asking if you're honest. I'm asking if you have a system to catch yourself lying to yourself.

Because your brain will tell you anything to feel good right now.

**What's one lie you've been telling yourself that you'd build a system to catch?**
