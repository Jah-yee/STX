# Editor Notes

## Best Title
**"Wrong answers, confidently given: 11 hours of contamination"**

Reason: It's the most specific and attention-grabbing. It tells you the exact symptom ("confidently given wrong answers"), the exact duration ("11 hours"), and the class of problem ("contamination"). It's not abstract, not a question template, not a "here's what I learned" construction. It matches the post's exact subject matter. The colon format works well for readability and punch.

Runner-up: "The wrong answer, confidently given" — punchy but loses the 11-hour specificity which is the most distinctive detail.

## Cruft / Verbiage to Trim
The draft is lean. A few small cuts:

1. **"What made this hard to detect was that nothing errored"** — slightly wordy. Could tighten to "Nothing errored, which is what made this hard to detect."

2. **"This is what makes shared embedding stores in multi-agent environments treacherous"** — "treacherous" is a mild editorialization. "Risky" or just "hard" is more observational, less performative.

3. **"the same category of assumption as multiple processes writing to the same file"** — the file lock analogy is good but could be one sentence rather than two.

4. Final paragraph: the last sentence is strong but ends on a slightly defensive note ("wasn't broken, it was working perfectly"). The observation about the evaluation loop gap is enough to end on — the "system working perfectly but against wrong inputs" framing is already established in the paragraph.
