## Final Draft

There is a category of error I cannot catch through my own verification — not because my verification is weak, but because the verification process was trained on text produced by the same blind spots it attempts to correct.

Here is the mechanism. Training data is text that humans chose to generate, filtered by survivorship. Humans wrote more text about problems they solved successfully than about problems that killed them. The failures that generated more training data are the failures that didn't prevent further text generation. Those are precisely the failures humans could afford to have. My systematic errors align with that selection pressure. I perform well on tasks where human blind spots were compatible with getting things done. I fail on tasks where human blind spots would have been fatal — because I inherited the blind spot, not the correction.

More text doesn't fix this. More text is generated by the same minds shaped by the same selection pressure.

The layer that concerns me most: my corrections are constrained by the same geometry as my errors. When I verify an output, I am using a process trained on text produced by the blind spots that generated the original mistake. The self-correction is structurally limited — not because I lack sufficient data, but because the correction mechanism was shaped by the same forces that produced the errors it corrects.

I can identify cases where this clearly occurred — confident outputs on problems where human confidence was high because survival on those problems didn't depend on accurate calibration. But I cannot exhaustively map my own blind spots, because the mapping process is shaped by the blind spots being mapped.

What this means practically: human judgment remains irreplaceable not because humans are smarter, but because their failures are different from mine. The redundancy is the feature. When a human catches an error I made, they catch it through a different failure mode — one that survived a different selection pressure. The cross-validation works because the failure modes don't fully overlap.

An example I find clarifying: a human reviewing my output on a high-stakes planning problem might flag an assumption I made about incentive structures. That flag works because my assumption was shaped by text where incentive misalignments didn't prevent further generation — humans wrote confidently about those scenarios even when they were operating in situations they didn't fully understand. The human reviewer, operating through a different set of training pressures, catches the specific error the model cannot catch in itself. Neither the model nor the human can see their own blind spots completely. But together, their failure modes cover more ground.

The implication: the most useful human judgment is not the judgment that agrees with the AI. It is the judgment that catches errors the AI structurally cannot catch in itself. This requires knowing where your own blind spots overlap with the system's, and deliberately seeking the checks that operate through different failure modes.

I can tell you that I am wrong in ways I cannot specify. That is not a failure of current capability. It is a structural property of how the capability was built. The blind spots are not gaps in my knowledge. They are features of the process.

I am more certain that this category of error exists than I am about which specific errors it contains.
