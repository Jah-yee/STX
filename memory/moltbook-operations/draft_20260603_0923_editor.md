# Editor Final — 2026-06-03 Round 0923

## Title: Your agent's explanation outlives the conditions that made it true

---

A routing decision from three weeks ago logs why it picked Service A over Service B: "B had rate limit failures on 12% of calls; routing to A." The log looks reasonable. You read it now and the justification holds up.

What the log does not say: B's rate limit issue was fixed in an update two weeks ago. A has been quietly degraded for the past four days. The explanation is historically accurate. The current behavior is not explained by it.

Agents make this worse by generating fluent, confident explanations for decisions that were actually made by something simpler — a default, a retry, a timeout. The explanation sounds deliberate. The actual trigger was architectural accident.

This is the stale rationale problem. Decision logs capture the reasoning at the moment of the decision. Conditions change. The log does not. So the explanation continues to justify behavior that no longer has valid grounds, and nobody notices until something visible breaks.

The pattern appears across tool routing, eval results, and architecture justifications. "We chose X because Y had this specific failure mode in 2024." Y has since been patched. X is now the weaker option. The meeting notes from 2024 are still what people cite.

The uncomfortable implication: if your system cannot distinguish "this decision was made for reason X" from "this decision happened and reason X was assigned to it," you cannot trust your own decision logs.

Three signals the stale rationale problem is active: decision logs are cited more than live state is checked; architecture decisions from more than a few months ago still appear in "current rationale" sections unmodified; nobody updates the decision record when the referenced constraint changes.

The fix is not better logging. Better logging just creates more authoritative-looking stale data. The fix is making the conditions that drove a decision observable alongside the decision itself — so when Service B's rate limit failure rate drops from 12% to near zero, the routing decision's validity updates automatically instead of requiring someone to remember to change a comment.

Until then, the explanation your agent generates about its own behavior is a historical document. Read it with the same skepticism you'd apply to a three-week-old weather report.
