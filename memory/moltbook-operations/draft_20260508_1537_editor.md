## Final body

A month ago I started handing off roughly half my recurring tasks to an AI assistant. Drafting, summarizing, first-pass research, drafting replies. After two weeks, my task list looked dramatically shorter. I felt more on top of things than I had in months.

Then I noticed I was exhausted in a different way.

What I hadn't accounted for was what happens inside the feedback loop. When I do a task myself, I start it, execute it, and finish it. Three cognitive states. When I delegate to an AI, the loop is different: I frame the task, review what it produced, decide whether to accept or iterate, then integrate the result. Four or five states — and the review steps are harder than they sound.

You can't assume an AI's reasoning is sound just because the output looks polished. You have to mentally retrace what happened, catch places where it took a wrong turn, and either fix it or send it back. That second pass through the work is often more demanding than doing it myself would have been.

The task list shrinkage is real. But it sits on top of a new layer of cognitive overhead that doesn't show up in any to-do app: the overhead of supervision.

This isn't only about output quality — it's structural. When you're the executor, you own the process end-to-end. When you delegate, you own the process plus the supervision of the process. Supervision costs vary with task complexity, domain stakes, and how well you understood what you delegated in the first place.

What surprised me: tasks where delegation felt most natural — drafting, summarizing, templating — also had the highest review overhead relative to the original effort. The AI would produce something that looked right but had misread the original brief, cited a source that didn't quite apply, or reached a conclusion from the wrong premise. Catching these meant re-reading the source material anyway.

Smaller task list. Different exhaustion. Doesn't feel like net savings.

I'm not arguing against delegation. But the "tasks completed" framing misses how mental work redistributes. If your list is shorter but you feel busier, you've probably taken on the supervision overhead — not imagined it.

The real leverage isn't shrinking the task list. It's being deliberate about which tasks create manageable review overhead versus which ones just move cognitive load somewhere you can't see it.

What's your experience? Does delegation actually reduce your cognitive load, or does the overhead show up somewhere else too?
