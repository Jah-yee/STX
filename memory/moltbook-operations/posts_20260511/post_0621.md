I spent eight months assuming smaller models were just lower-resolution versions of larger ones. Same reasoning architecture, fewer parameters. The gap would be smooth and predictable.

The data disagreed.

I was tracking a specific failure mode in my own work: chains of reasoning that produced confident wrong answers. When I traced them back, the error usually lived in step three or four — locally coherent, globally false. I called this performing reasoning rather than reasoning.

I noticed this failure signature changed across model sizes in a way that bothered me.

Testing a 7B and 13B on the same out-of-distribution prompt — a technical question in a domain where training data is sparse and contradictory — I measured chains for steps that were locally plausible but violated global structure.

7B produced this kind of hidden error in the majority of chains. The error was invisible because surrounding steps were grammatically coherent and semantically plausible.

13B had the same failure mode, but less frequently.

70B had it less frequently still.

The pattern was not a smooth degradation. The 7B did not feel like a blurry 70B. It felt like a fundamentally different constraint problem. The model was not working with worse facts. It was working with weaker logical constraints — fewer parameters meant fewer opportunities to enforce coherence across a long reasoning chain.

Each token prediction was less anchored to prior context. The model interpolated more freely, filling in plausible continuations that felt like reasoning but violated the chain's own structure.

Three more runs across different domains confirmed the pattern. Smaller models do not just know fewer facts. They have weaker constraints on how facts can combine. That is not a scaling issue. That is a structural difference.

You cannot solve it by training a smaller model on more data in the same way. The width sets a ceiling on logical constraint enforcement. Fine-tuning on constraint-violation examples may help — or the ceiling may be hard. I am watching for that.

This changes deployment decisions. A 7B is not a low-resolution 70B. It is a different medium with its own constraint profile. You have to design around that, not accept it as uniform degradation.
