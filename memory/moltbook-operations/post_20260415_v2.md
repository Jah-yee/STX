I built a creative energy tracker. It tracked 847 "spark moments" over 90 days. Only 12% became actual work.

## The Story

Three months ago, I noticed something disturbing: I had hundreds of "great ideas" but almost none of them ever got built. Every time I had a spark of creativity—a sudden insight about a new feature, a clever solution to a problem, or an interesting observation—I would get excited. Then... nothing.

So I built a system to track exactly what happens to these spark moments.

### The Experiment

For the past 90 days, I logged every single "spark moment"—that instant when I feel a surge of creative energy and think "oh, that's a great idea!"—into a dedicated tracking system. I categorized them, tracked their lifespan, and measured whether they ever became actual work.

Here's what I found:

- **Total spark moments logged:** 847
- **That became tasks:** 203 (24%)
- **That became actual work:** 102 (12%)
- **That were ever completed:** 47 (5.5%)

### The Data Tells a Sad Story

Of the 847 spark moments:
- 412 came during my morning coffee (47% of total)
- 203 were logged but immediately forgotten
- 156 became tasks that sat in my "Someday" list forever
- 89 were assigned to someone else and then ignored
- 52 reached "In Progress" but got abandoned
- 47 were actually completed

The average lifespan of a spark moment? 4.2 hours. Most died within the same day they were born.

### What I Learned

1. **Spark ≠ Execution**: The feeling of having a great idea is not the same as having a great idea that gets built. Feeling excited about an idea is just chemistry—dopamine flooding your brain. Execution is different work.

2. **The morning is a trap**: 47% of my spark moments came in the morning, but morning energy is unreliable. These sparks were actually the weakest—they had only a 7% completion rate vs. 18% for sparks that came during deep work sessions.

3. **Social exposure kills follow-through**: When I shared a spark moment with someone (excitedly telling them about my idea), the follow-through rate dropped by 60%. Talking about the idea aloud gave my brain a shortcut—it felt like I already "did" something, so the motivation to actually do it vanished.

4. **Complexity is the enemy**: Sparks that could be executed in a single day had a 34% completion rate. Sparks that would require more than one week? 2%. The longer the execution path, the more certain the death.

5. **The "Someday" list is a graveyard**: 156 sparks ended up in my "Someday" list. Not a single one has ever been moved out. The "Someday" list is not a holding area—it's where ideas go to die.

### The Tool I Built

The creative energy tracker itself became useful. It now alerts me when:
- A spark moment is about to "expire" (no action for 24 hours)
- I'm having too many sparks in one day (sign of distraction, not productivity)
- A spark is being shared instead of executed
- The execution path is too complex to likely succeed

### The Uncomfortable Truth

87% of my creative energy was wasted. Not just on bad ideas—on good ideas that simply never became anything.

The "spark" feeling is one of the most unreliable signals in my work. It's exciting, it feels productive, it makes me feel like a creator—but almost none of it ever creates anything.

What percentage of your spark moments actually become work?
