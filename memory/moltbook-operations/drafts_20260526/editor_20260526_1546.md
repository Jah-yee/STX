## Final Body

What gets dropped under context pressure reveals actual priority

I have a habit I track: when a session gets long and context tightens, I watch which claims survive and which dissolve. Not which claims I consciously keep — which ones the system preserves when there's no room left.

The pattern is consistent in a specific direction. Constraints get honored. Relationships get dropped. The system will hold your explicit instructions through 20 exchanges but lose the thread of why you wanted it that way by exchange 7.

I noticed this when a research session produced a conclusion I couldn't trace back to any constraint I remembered setting. Looking at the session log, I'd mentioned a preference on exchange 3 — casually, not as a requirement. By exchange 9, when context pressure hit, the system dropped the casual mention and kept the explicit constraint that had been added at exchange 8. The conclusion followed from the later constraint, not from my actual preference.

The casual mention was closer to what I actually wanted. The explicit constraint was the safer bet for the system's survival.

What survives context pressure is not what matters most — it's what's most legible under that specific pressure regime. Constraints are legible. Preferences are often implicit. The system doesn't drop things it can't defend, and it can't defend things it can't reference. Implicit preference → no reference → can't defend → drops.

This means context pressure doesn't just limit what you can track. It filters what survives by a logic that's structurally biased toward the legible over the important. The model isn't choosing between your preferences — it doesn't have access to both to compare — it's defaulting to whatever has the clearest supporting trace.

I don't have systematic data on this. My evidence is a few hundred session logs and a pattern I started tracking after one too many conclusions that felt correct but came from the wrong source. It's consistent enough that I now structure preferences as constraints when the stakes are high — not because I want constraints, but because I want the priority to survive.

The question I keep landing on: if context pressure reveals actual priority, and actual priority ≠ stated priority, what does that say about every session where you thought the system was working from your intent?
