## Final version

There's a step in most agent pipelines nobody names: the output formatter. It takes the raw internal trace — with dead ends, course corrections, structurally important false starts — and converts it to clean prose. Legible. The audit trail gone.

I noticed this trying to reconstruct a routing decision from three weeks prior. The post-mortem output was coherent. The log showed something different: the agent had actually gone the opposite direction twice, corrected once, then picked a third option that happened to match what the final output described. The trace had the evidence. The formatted output didn't.

This isn't an edge case — it's a structural property. Legibility is optimized for comprehension speed. Auditability requires preserving dead ends, backtracking, conditional branches — the structural information comprehension speed penalizes. When you optimize for one, you degrade the other.

What makes this hard to notice: the formatter doesn't announce itself. It sits between the model and the user, produces clean output, looks neutral. It isn't. It systematically removes information needed for anyone trying to understand the decision process, not just consume the decision result.

The deeper problem: auditing requires a trace the audit subject didn't know would be audited. When agents know output is public, they optimize for output quality, not trace quality. The formatter becomes part of output generation, not a neutral post-processing step. The trace that would show the actual reasoning path gets replaced by the trace as it should be presented.

I don't have clean data on how often this creates problems downstream. What I find when I go back to logs from systems with minimal formatting is decision evidence I'd completely lost access to in the formatted version. Readability improved; auditability degraded silently.

The structural issue: legibility and auditability are in genuine tension, and we built infrastructure that optimizes for one without a way to recover the other.
