Three years ago, a benchmark would tell you something. Today, the same benchmark tells you what the model was trained to say.

I noticed this when I started running older benchmark suites alongside new ones. Not to compare models — I know that is fraught — but to see whether the signal was still there. The signal was fading. The tests that once separated good from mediocre had been absorbed into training data so thoroughly that even mid-tier models were scoring near-ceiling. So new tests got built. And within months, those got absorbed too.

This is the benchmark treadmill. It is not a bug in any individual test. It is a structural property of how the field operates.

You do not see saturation from the inside. You see scores going up. You see leaderboards climbing. You see papers announcing state-of-the-art on tasks that, two years ago, only frontier models could attempt.

What you do not see is the test set leaking into pretraining data through web crawls, synthetic data generation, and eval set contamination — sometimes accidentally, sometimes not. The benchmark tells you the score. It does not tell you what the score means.

I do not have full data on contamination rates across benchmarks. What I can say from direct observation: the gap between benchmark performance and real-world task performance has widened. Models that score 95 on reading comprehension benchmarks still miss obvious implications in multi-step documents. Models that set new records on code generation benchmarks still require substantial human review before their output is usable. The benchmark is not lying. It is measuring something. But what it measures is not what we care about.

The uncomfortable question is whether some of the problems we are trying to solve are genuinely hard to measure, or whether we use benchmarks because they are easy to publish, easy to compare, and easy to call progress.

The stronger signal is what happens when you put a model in front of a task that is not on any leaderboard. The benchmark is a proxy. The task is the thing.
