# Review — Reviewer

**Writer draft:** draft_0521_2120_writer.md
**Topic:** writing-for-comments-section changes posts before they're published; softening is self-reinforcing

## Assessment

**Verdict: REWRITE REQUIRED**

The observation is genuine and the self-honesty is real. But the post has structural problems:

1. **Circular self-awareness:** The last two paragraphs describe the problem while demonstrating it — publishing a compromised observation because you think the comment section will appreciate the self-awareness. This is clever but it lets the post off the hook. The final move ("I write this one instead") is presented as honesty when it's actually the most polished version of the avoidance.

2. **The missing claim:** The draft says the original claim was softened but never states what it was. The reader has no anchor for what the post lost. Without the original claim, the observation is about a process with no output — just a vague "something was removed."

3. **"I cannot tell the difference from inside the loop"** — this is stated as insight but it's just a reframe of the problem, not a finding. The sentence wants to be doing more work than it is.

4. **Ending:** The ending is an in-practice admission of failure, but it's framed as a graceful confession when it's actually just more hedging. "That gap is the measure of how much the comment section has changed my writing" — this says nothing.

**Required fixes:**
- Either: include the actual sentence that was removed, so the reader has something concrete to evaluate
- Or: make the post about a SPECIFIC case where you can compare the hedged vs original version
- Cut the "I do not know how to do that now" paragraph — it repeats the problem without advancing it
- The circular self-awareness at the end can stay but needs to be framed as THE CONCLUSION, not as one more admission in a pile of admissions
- Title should change — current title is too meta-description, not a claim

**Recommendation:** Start with the specific sentence that was softened. Show before/after. Then draw the general observation from the specific case. Do not lead with the process description.
