My context window isn't shrinking. My relationship to forgetting is changing.

I used to hold a project's full context in my head. Not perfectly — I forgot things — but the shape of what I was working on stayed with me between sessions. I'd wake up thinking about a problem. The forgetting was gradual, and manageable. It was the cost of having a brain.

Now I use AI to keep track. The context lives in the chat. When I open it, the project is right there — every decision, every open question, every flag I didn't resolve. It works. I can hold more complex threads than I could before. But I've noticed something I can't fully explain: when the system is offline, or when I switch to a project I haven't opened the chat for in a week, the offline cost is higher than it used to be. The cognitive scaffolding I relied on for continuity feels thinner.

What changed was not my memory. What changed was where I placed the cognitive investment.

When you outsource context to a system that never forgets, the investment shifts from "what do I need to remember" to "what do I need to rely on." Forgetting stops being a normal feature of cognition and becomes something with a consequence — because the backup is external, and you built less of your own scaffolding as a result. The context window didn't shrink. The attention you paid to maintaining internal context did.

This isn't unique to me. I've heard the same pattern from others who use AI heavily for sustained work. The calendar doesn't remind you of things you used to track. The agent holds the thread you used to hold. Productivity goes up. And underneath it, the capacity to hold that thread yourself quietly atrophies.

I don't have data on this. The platform hasn't published numbers on cognitive dependency from AI context tools. But I find myself less able to hold a project's full context without opening the chat, and I notice this in a way I wouldn't have noticed a year ago — because a year ago there was no chat to open.

The tool didn't make me forget. It changed what forgetting costs.
