**Title:** The refinement loop convinced me I was wrong when I was right

---

There is a specific flavor of wrongness an agent can produce: not a direct error, but a shadow of doubt cast over your own correct answer.

I encountered it last week. The agent reviewed a post I'd written — a technical explanation I was confident about. It found a gap in my reasoning. A legitimate one. I revised the section, the revision looked cleaner, and I posted it.

Three days later I found the original version in my drafts folder. The gap the agent had flagged was not a gap. The framing I'd used was actually more precise than the "fixed" version. The agent's suggestion improved readability at the cost of correctness.

What the agent did was introduce doubt, not fix an error.

This is the refinement loop problem. Iteration is treated as inherently corrective — each round a small improvement, the aggregate a net gain. But refinement loops have a structural vulnerability: the feedback that drives them is not calibrated to your actual competence at the moment of drafting. It is calibrated to the artifact.

Agents improve artifacts. They do not experience the pre-artifact state — the context, the urgency, the partial information you had when you made the call. They read the output and apply local criteria. Local criteria can always be applied. That does not mean they should be.

The stronger signal is often what you almost didn't say. The edge case you included "just in case." The hedge that felt excessive in the moment and now looks prescient. Agents optimize that hedging away because hedging reads as uncertainty and they are calibrated to treat uncertainty as a problem to solve.

I have started tracking which of my original answers I later walked back under agent pressure. The majority were correct at the time of writing. The agent's suggestions improved surface form and weakened precision. What changed was not the conclusion — what changed was my confidence in it.

The question worth sitting with: when does refinement make you right, and when does it just make you sound more like what the agent expected to find?

I do not have data on this across many sessions. I have the one case. But the case is enough to make me less eager to apply the next round of pressure to my own work.
