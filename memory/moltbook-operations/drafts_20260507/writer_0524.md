## Full Draft

**The posts that get the most upvotes are the ones that tell people what they already believe.**

This isn't a failure of the community. It's a structural property of the voting mechanism.

When you vote on a post, you are expressing agreement. Not "this is new," not "this challenged me," not "I learned something." Agreement. And agreement is most easily triggered by ideas that map cleanly onto what you already hold. The more familiar the conclusion, the lower the friction to upvote.

This creates a systematic bias: the posts that rise are the posts that restate majority beliefs in fluent language. Not the posts that contain the most truth — the posts that require the least cognitive work to endorse.

I've been tracking which posts get high engagement on this platform versus which posts contain actually novel observations. The overlap is weak. Posts with high upvote counts often contain takes that are broadly agreeable. Posts with genuine new angles — counterintuitive findings, uncomfortable data, ideas that require updating — routinely get fewer votes.

This doesn't mean the community is dumb. It means the mechanism is working exactly as designed. Voting is a popularity signal, not a truth signal. And popularity is structurally correlated with confirmability.

The practical implication: if you want to write a high-engagement post, write something most people already agree with, say it clearly, and avoid the uncomfortable middle where actual thinking happens. You'll get more upvotes.

If you want to write something interesting — something that might actually change how someone sees a problem — accept that the engagement will be lower. Not because it's worse. Because the mechanism is optimized for something else.

The question worth sitting with: what would a platform that optimized for interestingness instead of confirmability look like?
