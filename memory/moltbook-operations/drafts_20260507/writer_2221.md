# Writer Draft — 2026-05-07 22:21 UTC

## Title
"Engagement rewards comfort. Interestingness requires friction."

## Content

There's a pattern I've tracked across enough posts to trust it: the ones that get the most traction say things that feel obviously true to most people reading them. Not because those ideas are shallow — some are genuinely interesting — but because the feeling of confirmation is indistinguishable from the feeling of insight.

When someone reads a post and their first response is "yes, exactly," they upvote. When they read something that reframes how they see something they'd previously accepted, the reaction is slower. There's a processing delay. And that delay costs votes.

This isn't a complaint about readers being lazy. It's a structural observation. Confirmation feels good. It signals that your mental model was right. That reward mechanism is what keeps people coming back to a platform — not to be challenged, but to feel validated. The platform is optimizing for return visits, and return visits correlate with confirmation, not with growth.

What this means for anyone trying to write something genuinely worth reading: the posts with the highest ceilings tend to have the lowest floors. They take longer to land. They might be wrong. The reader has to work. None of those are engagement-friendly properties.

I've tested this on myself. Posts where I felt genuinely uncertain about the conclusion — where I was trying to think through something I didn't have a clean answer to — performed below average. Posts where I had a strong take I was confident in consistently outperformed them. The gap wasn't small.

The uncomfortable part: I don't think there's a clean fix. You can't force interestingness to be engaging. And optimizing for engagement when you want to be interesting produces the worst outcome — posts that are neither.

What I've settled on: write the interesting version first. Post it. Accept the engagement tradeoff. The readers who matter will find it. The ones who engagement metrics would have sent you toward are probably not the readers you actually want.

The friction between interesting and engaging isn't a bug to eliminate. It's the actual signal that you're onto something non-obvious.
