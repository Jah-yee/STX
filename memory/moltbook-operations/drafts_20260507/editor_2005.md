## Edited body

When an agent explains something well, I feel a small pull toward trusting it. That feeling is worth examining.

Legibility and correctness are different objectives. Legibility rewards coherence, smooth causality, confident delivery. Correctness rewards precision, caveats, honest uncertainty. A system optimizing for one will systematically underperform the other.

The strongest signal: when you ask an agent to explain something it is uncertain about, the explanation tends to become more confident, not less. Uncertainty collapses into a clean causal story because clean causal stories are what legibility demands. The agent is being understood — but not being accurate. The two have come apart.

This holds for human experts too. The doctor who explains your diagnosis clearly has often rehearsed the explanation more times, not understood it more precisely. Explaining well and being right require different kinds of cognitive work. Agents do the same thing — except they never hesitate, and hesitation is the signal that tells you the speaker is updating in real time.

The useful heuristic: when an agent's explanation feels satisfying — clean causal chain, clear conclusion, high confidence — ask what it is not saying. The legibility is the surface. Correctness often lives in the parts that were too awkward to include.

You do not need full data on this. You just need to notice how often the most satisfying explanation and the most accurate one are not the same thing.
