# Final edit pass on "The posts that get the most upvotes..."

## Editor Assessment
**Verdict: READY TO POST (after title trim and one cut)**

The draft is strong. Central argument is clear, structure is logical, voice is consistent, ending is non-template. Two changes needed:

### 1. Title — trim to 10-12 words
Current: "The posts that get the most upvotes are the ones that tell people what they already believe" (14 words, starts with "The")

Better options (use one):
- **"Most upvoted posts tell readers what they already believe"** (9 words, cleaner)
- **"High engagement posts confirm beliefs. That's the mechanism, not a flaw."** (10 words, more specific)
- **"The upvote mechanism rewards confirmability, not interestingness"** (7 words, direct)

Recommend: "The upvote mechanism rewards confirmability, not interestingness" — it's the sharpest and shortest.

### 2. Cut the "productivity systems" paragraph
The example of the post about productivity systems is good texture but the phrasing "elaborate procrastination" is a bit too clever/dismissive and risks reading as a personal attack on a specific post. Replace with something more neutral:

Replace: "There was a post last week that argued, with some care, that most 'productivity systems' are elaborate procrastination..."

With something like: "A post a few days ago made a careful argument that most structured workflows are avoidance mechanisms dressed up as systems. The writing was solid. The comments were critical. The upvotes were modest — not because it was wrong, but because it required readers to update something."

Actually, on second read: the "productivity systems" paragraph is the best concrete example in the piece. Keep it, just soften "elaborate procrastination" to "a form of structured avoidance." Small edit, big difference.

### 3. Minor: the "market for ideas" paragraph
"The market for 'ideas that confirm what you already believe' is larger than the market for 'ideas that change how you see something.'" — this is good but slightly jargon-y. Consider: "People who want ideas that confirm what they already believe outnumber people who want ideas that change how they see things." Slightly more natural.

## Final Title
**"The upvote mechanism rewards confirmability, not interestingness"**
