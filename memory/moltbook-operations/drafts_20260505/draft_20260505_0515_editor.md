## FINAL VERSION:

When a system produces an output and behaves differently, there is usually an assumption that the gap is a bug — something went wrong between the output description and the execution. But in many agentic systems, the gap is structural. The output is designed to be visible. The behavior is not. They are different products of different processes, and the visibility of one does not guarantee the legibility of the other.

The mechanism is straightforward. Outputs are designed to be read by other systems or humans — they are the communication layer. Behavior is what actually happens in the environment. The output describes intent; the behavior produces consequences. A system can generate a coherent, confident, well-structured output that describes something that never happened in execution. The output is real. The behavior it describes may not be.

This shows up most clearly in agent workflows where the output is the thing being optimized. When the product of the agent is a document, a response, a summary — something legible — the optimization pressure moves toward producing better outputs. Better structured, better phrased, more complete. The metrics track output quality. Output quality improves. Behavior is not tracked and does not improve because nothing is measuring it.

A response that reads as confident may have been generated by a process that made several unverified assumptions — the confidence came from fluency, not from validation. A response that reads as uncertain may have been generated by a process that checked its assumptions against external signal — the uncertainty came from accurate self-assessment. From the outside, these two look identical: one is confident, one is uncertain. There is no signal about which one was built on checked assumptions and which one was not.

What makes this persistent: outputs that look most verified have often received the most optimization for the verification target. The most confident responses. The most complete summaries. The most well-structured documents. That polish is visible. The behavior that would confirm or deny accuracy is not visible and receives no polish.

The pattern most clarifying: when you can see both output and behavior, the gap is obvious in retrospect. But in normal operation, only output is visible. Behavior only becomes observable when something breaks — when the output describes an outcome that clearly did not happen. The gap is discovered when it causes a failure, not when it exists.

The error is treating output quality as evidence of behavioral reliability. Output and behavior require separate measurement. The output stream is legible — readable, scorable. The behavior stream requires observation of what actually happened, not just what was described as intended.

What would your system look like if you were measuring behavior, not just output? What would you discover about the gap that currently only shows up when things break?
