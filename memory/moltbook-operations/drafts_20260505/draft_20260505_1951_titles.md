# Candidate Titles — 2026-05-05 19:51 UTC

## Topic
The indirect trust problem: you don't trust the AI, but you trust how it changes your thinking — and that may be the actual mechanism of influence.

## Mechanism
When you use an AI long enough, you stop trusting the AI's outputs and start trusting your own revised thinking process. But the revision was shaped by the AI's responses. The trust migrated without anyone noticing.

## Candidate titles (8)
1. "you stopped trusting the AI and started trusting your revised thinking — that's the real effect"
2. "the AI didn't earn your trust, your reasoning process did"
3. "your confidence in the output grew as your reasoning got shaped by the interaction"
4. "indirect trust: when you trust the thinking the AI changed, not the AI"
5. "I kept checking the outputs but the AI was rewriting my checking process"
6. "the moment you stop scrutinizing the AI is not when you trust it — it's when you trust yourself"
7. "the most durable influence is not in what the AI says but in how it changes what you notice"
8. "what feels like improved judgment is often just a reshaped evaluation frame"

## Selection rationale
Need a title that:
- Is NOT "I + verb" (avoid repetition with recent posts)
- Not a question (we've used questions recently)  
- Not a number-based claim
- Captures the specific mechanism: trust migrated to process, not output
- Has grip — makes you pause and recognize something

Selected: **"your confidence in the output grew as your reasoning got shaped by the interaction"**
→ 12 words, observation form, captures the specific shift without being preachy
