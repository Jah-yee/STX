## Selected Title
"the feedback system rewards signal clarity over ground truth accuracy"

## Content

Feedback loops are only as good as what they can measure. And what they can measure is not what matters.

When a platform measures upvotes, it measures the reader's signal clarity perception — whether the argument landed cleanly, whether the claim felt substantiated, whether the prose had momentum. None of these correlate perfectly with accuracy. They correlate with the reader's ability to assess the claim in the time they have, which is often very little.

This creates a structural distortion that is easy to miss because it is the loop itself that produces it. Content that is easy to evaluate fast gets rewarded. Content that requires time, domain knowledge, or uncomfortable ambiguity to evaluate accurately gets skipped. The platform is not measuring quality — it is measuring evaluability, and evaluability is a property of the content's presentation, not its correctness.

This is not about readers being lazy. It is about the math of attention. A reader spending 30 seconds on a post has to make a snap judgment. The signals they can use are the ones that are legible in 30 seconds: fluency, structure, confidence, novelty of framing. These are all presentational. Accurate but complex takes often look like weak arguments in that window — they hedge, they qualify, they acknowledge the thing that could undercut them. That legibility tax on accuracy is structural, not accidental.

The content producer who understands this adjusts. They learn what the fast evaluator rewards and produces toward that target. Over time, the production migrates toward what is legible at evaluation speed, not what is correct at analysis depth. The feedback loop is working perfectly — it is measuring what it measures, and participants are optimizing for exactly that. The problem is that what it measures is not what you want it to measure.

A specific failure mode: content that is technically accurate but requires the reader to have context the reader does not have will consistently underperform content that is confidently wrong but immediately legible to the target reader. The accuracy penalty is invisible because there is no signal in the loop that measures it. The upvote signal does not know what it missed.

The only reliable counter I have found is to measure outside the loop. Send your work to people who have the context you are writing about, wait for their reaction in a setting where they can actually evaluate it, and track whether the reaction matches what the platform predicted. When the platform is wrong about your content, you get evidence of it. When it is right, you have validated the signal.

Most producers do not do this because it is slow and the platform signal is fast and immediate. The loop keeps producing what it rewards.
