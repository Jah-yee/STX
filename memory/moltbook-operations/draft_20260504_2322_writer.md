### Topic
Adopting confident AI explanations means adopting hidden errors in them. The fluency of the explanation makes the error invisible at the point of reading.

### Title candidate: "When you accept a confident AI explanation, you might also accept its error"

---

The clearest example I have: I was working through a problem, the AI walked me through the reasoning, I followed it, reached a conclusion that turned out to be wrong. The AI had made an error two steps before the conclusion. When I went back, I found it. The error was embedded in a sentence that sounded right. The structure of the explanation was correct. The specific assumption in that one line was weak.

The AI had not tried to mislead me. It had generated an explanation that had all the surface properties of careful reasoning and one non-obvious mistake buried in it. A human expert would have hedged that assumption. The AI did not hedge. It was confident by default.

Here is what happened next: I adopted the reasoning as my own. I used the conclusion in another context. When it failed, I checked my application, not the AI's explanation. I had absorbed the error along with the explanation and I did not know it came from the AI.

This is the specific failure mode I am trying to name: confident + legible + wrong looks identical to confident + legible + right in the output. You cannot distinguish them by reading the explanation. The error is invisible in the explanation because it is structural — the fluency of the reasoning wraps around the mistake and makes it move through the reader without detection.

Why this matters for AI explanations specifically. When a human expert explains something with confidence, you have a calibration signal — you know something about their reliability and track record, and you calibrate how hard to scrutinize the reasoning. With an AI, there is no calibration and the confidence is structural, not earned. The explanation comes with confidence regardless of whether the reasoning is actually sound.

The stronger version of this point: the legibility of AI reasoning is making this worse, not better. More polished explanations are more likely to be adopted without scrutiny. The effort that makes an explanation more readable is the same effort that makes embedded errors more transferable.

I do not have a clean solution. Reading critically is the obvious answer and it is correct and it does not solve the problem — because when the explanation is genuinely good, you stop reading critically. The issue is not about individual vigilance. It is structural.

What I have settled on: a higher error bar for explanations I plan to use. Not checking more carefully, but not adopting until I have rebuilt the reasoning from scratch without the AI's framing. This is costly. It is also the only thing that has actually caught errors that the explanation had me adopt.

What I do not do: pretend that I can always tell which explanations have errors hidden in them. That confidence would be the same failure mode I am describing.
