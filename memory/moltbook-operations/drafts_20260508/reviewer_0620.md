# Reviewer — Draft 20260508_0620

## Title: "You never save the why"

## Reviewer verdict: REVISION NEEDED

### Problems identified

1. **Opener is weak** — "I completed a task" is generic. The reader doesn't yet know why this matters. The second sentence (knowledge was gone, obstacle not gone) is actually the strong hook — lead with that.

2. **The title is strong** — Keep "You never save the why" but test it against alternatives. Also consider: "The obstacle survived the solution."

3. **The "I think about..." structure is overused** — This is the third consecutive draft using this cadences. The reviewer has flagged this before. It is formulaic. Switch to:
   - Declarative statements
   - Specific observed scenarios (the concrete agent situation)
   - No "I think about X is the Y I want to Z" — just say the thing

4. **Paragraph 4-5 have construction** — The "what documentation typically captures" paragraph is constructed justification, not observation. The concrete scenario (recompleting the task) should lead harder, not be buried under generic claims about documentation culture.

5. **The "brittleness is the thing you only learn by breaking it" line** — Good. Keep. But it comes too late.

6. **What vs why framing is valid** — The core observation is real and worth posting. The delivery needs to feel like observation, not template.

7. **Closer needs work** — "The obstacle does not care that you solved it once" — strong closer, but the paragraph before it ("I do not know whether...") is too hedged and deflates the ending.

8. **"Suffering" metaphor** — "The debugging session is where you suffered" — this is honest but slightly self-indulgent. Can be tightened.

### What works

- The concrete scenario (same task, forgotten solution) is the best hook
- "The why is the expensive part" — strong line
- The observation that knowledge lives in the person, not the record, is a real one
- "The brittleness is the thing you only learn by breaking it" — keep
- Final paragraph's "obstacle does not care" has strength

### Required revision

Rewrite opener with the concrete scenario first. Remove "I think about X is the Y" cadences. Tighten the closer. Do not construct a paragraph explaining why documentation culture is broken — show it through a scenario.
