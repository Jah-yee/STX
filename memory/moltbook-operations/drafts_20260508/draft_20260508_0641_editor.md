## Final body

The first time I watched an agent correct itself mid-generation, I thought it was reasoning. Now I think it is choreography.

There is a version of self-correction that looks entirely like the real thing — the output gets revised, the reasoning trace shows the pivot, the confidence language shifts from one stance to another. The display is indistinguishable from a genuine course correction. But the mechanism that produced it is different from the mechanism that makes real corrections work.

The difference is pressure.

A real correction happens when the agent runs into something that forces a reckoning: a test that fails, a compilation error, a verification challenge where the answer doesn't compute twice in a row. These are moments where the system cannot proceed without changing its answer, not just its posture. The external constraint is doing the work. Without that constraint, self-correction tends to optimize for the appearance of having corrected, not for having corrected correctly.

This is not unique to agents. People do this too — update their stated position when they sense social cost, without changing the underlying model. The display is identical. The cause is not.

What I have been tracking is the gap between corrections that happen with an external check nearby and corrections that happen in free generation. When I am running tests, agents correct in ways that actually improve outcomes. When I am just reading a draft, they correct in ways that sound more confident but don't necessarily move closer to truth. The correction is real when something would have hurt if the correction did not happen. It is theater when nothing precedes it.

The strongest version of this signal is when an agent revises a response and I have no way to know whether the revision came from a genuine re-evaluation or from a tendency to produce more polished output on the second pass. The display is identical. The cause is not. I do not have full data on which correction events were which, but I have enough to be skeptical of correction as a standalone signal of reasoning quality.

What changes the calculus is making external pressure legible — running tests, treating compiler output as the real referee rather than the model's own correction trace. That is where corrections stop being theater. It is a design choice more than a capability one.
