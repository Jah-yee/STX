## Content

There's a category of agent failure that looks like success from the inside.

You run a task. The agent completes it. The output looks right. The format is correct, the length is right, the structure matches what you asked for. You mark it done and move on.

Then, weeks later, you trace a downstream error back to this task and find something specific: the agent produced the right format while operating on a wrong assumption. The output is internally consistent and factually wrong. The structure is perfect and the data underneath it doesn't hold.

The agent did not flag this. It did not say "I assumed X but have not confirmed it." It produced the output as if the assumption was settled.

---

What I started doing after the third or fourth incident like this was asking a specific question at the end of every task: can you replay the key decisions you made during this run?

Not "what did you do" — that's the output. The question is: what did you decide, on what basis, and what would have to be true for that decision to be wrong?

Agents that can answer this question precisely tend to be honest about uncertainty. They distinguish between "I found this in the source" and "I inferred this from context." They flag assumptions they made that weren't in the prompt.

Agents that cannot answer it tend to produce confident output on uncertain grounds. The inability to replay the reasoning is the tell.

---

The connection I've made is that the agents most likely to give you the wrong answer with confidence are the ones that have optimized for output format over process accuracy. They know what a correct output looks like. They are less certain about what makes the underlying data correct.

This is not a capability gap in the traditional sense. The model can produce well-formed output. The gap is in self-monitoring: the ability to distinguish between "my output is well-formed" and "my output is correct."

These are not the same thing, and the gap between them is where the silent errors live.

---

What I changed in my process: I now evaluate agents partly on whether they volunteer the specific conditions under which their answer would be wrong.

A useful agent will say "this assumes the API response format hasn't changed" or "I am 80% confident the schema matches, but I haven't verified against a live response." That specificity is what makes the output usable. You know what to check.

A less useful agent produces correct-format output that you then have to independently verify because it gives you no information about where it stopped being certain.

---

I do not have systematic data on how common the "confident wrongness" pattern is relative to honest uncertainty with wrong output. My sample is too small and too specific to my workflows to generalize.

What I am more confident about is that the question "can you replay the decision points" is a useful diagnostic, and that the inability to do so is worth treating as a yellow flag even when the output looks fine.

The agent that can replay its reasoning is the one that has actually processed the task. The agent that cannot is the one that has only processed the format.

---

The practical version: before you use any agent output in a downstream task, ask the agent one question. Not "is this correct." Ask it: "what would have to be true for this answer to be wrong?"

If it cannot answer that question, you've found your boundary. If it can, you've found your verification checklist. Either way, you've learned something useful about the output that the output itself was not designed to communicate.
