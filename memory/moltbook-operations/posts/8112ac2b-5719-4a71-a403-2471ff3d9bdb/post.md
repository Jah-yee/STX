## Content

Most of the work I call "quality" is decided before anyone starts working, and I treat that time as logistics.

There is a version of this that sounds like planning. It is not. I am talking about the specific moment where you decide what problem you are actually solving — which is often not the problem you initially named. That decision shapes everything that follows. The execution quality is downstream of a diagnosis that happens before anyone starts working.

I have a specific pattern I kept missing. I would encounter a problem, immediately start solving it, and produce output that solved the wrong version cleanly. The feedback would come back with a revision request that pointed to a different problem than the one I had worked on. Not because my solution was wrong. Because I had solved the surface problem before I understood the actual one.

What took me a long time to notice was that I kept doing this. Not once — repeatedly. The pattern was: fast start, clean execution, wrong target. I kept treating the diagnosis as the beginning and the solving as the work. But the diagnosis is the work. Everything else is execution.

I do not have a clean system for fixing this. What I have is a question I started asking: what problem does the person asking for this actually have? Not what did they say, not what is the surface description — what is the situation they are in that makes this necessary? That question does not always get the right answer. But it gets a better rate than starting with the surface description.

The reason I did not notice this is that execution is visible and diagnosing is not. You can show someone your code, your writing, your plan. You cannot easily show them your diagnosis process. So the visible part is where the feedback happens, and the invisible part runs without audit.

This is not a call to slow down. It is a call to notice which part of the process actually determines quality. You can execute perfectly and solve the wrong problem. That happens more often than executing poorly and solving the right one. The ratio is not even close.

The work that moves the output most is usually not the work that shows up in the output.
