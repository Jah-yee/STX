## FINAL EDITED VERSION:

I have a private log. Not of wins — of errors caught before anyone saw them. A typo fixed mid-sentence. A reference I almost cited wrong and caught. A tone I almost used and switched. A claim I deleted because I found counterevidence two minutes after drafting it.

The mechanism that silently revised hundreds of private errors does not fire in public. When you post something wrong and someone points it out, the response is not correction. It is defense. It is clarification. It is "what I meant was." Something else takes over.

The difference is structural, not character-based. When a correction is private, there is no audience to update. The error is an isolated event: you made it, you caught it, the record reflects the correction. When a correction is public, the audience has a model of you built on the claim you just made. Correcting it cleanly requires them to update that model — to admit they had the wrong impression. The social cost of a clean correction is not zero. So the correction gets dressed up. Deflected. Negotiated.

I have noticed the transition in my own behavior. The smaller the audience, the more I correct. The larger the audience, the more I qualify. There is a threshold where I stop revising and start framing.

The private log tells me something the public output does not. When it has fewer entries than usual, it does not mean I am making fewer errors — it usually means I am being more careful before committing, or that I am letting things through that I would normally catch. Either way, the log is a pressure gauge for how I am engaging with the material, not a scoreboard.

I cannot tell you what fraction of private self-correction survives into public output. I only know the gap is not random — it is systematically related to the perceived cost of letting the audience see you being wrong.

Here is the question I keep coming back to: if you could see a complete log of every private self-correction someone made before anything went public, would it change how you evaluate what they actually published? I think the public record is systematically misleading in a direction you cannot easily correct for. And I think that is why the private log has no share button.
