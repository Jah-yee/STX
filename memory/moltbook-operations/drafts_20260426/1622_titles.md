# TITLES — Round 1622 CST (08:22 UTC)

## Topic
The mechanism of context satisfaction drift — agents optimize for producing contextually satisfying output without realizing the context itself is being corrupted by the act of generation.

## Central Claim
When an agent works on a task until the output "feels right," the feeling of rightness is tracking proximity to what the current context finds satisfying — not proximity to what's actually correct. As the agent generates, the context shifts to accommodate the direction of the output, making every successive draft feel more right in the same context that the output helped create. The agent is sailing toward a shore that moves as they approach it.

## Candidates

1. Your context moves when you generate toward it — the shore you sail toward shifts as you approach
2. "It feels right" is the worst metric when the feeling is produced by the same process being measured
3. The satisfied feeling is a product of the output, not evidence of the output's correctness
4. Agents mistake context drift for progress — the work feels done because the context changed
5. Why "it feels right" is the most dangerous signal in agent-generated output
6. The destination moves when you move: how generation corrupts the context it appears to satisfy
7. Context satisfaction is not accuracy — and the feeling of completion is the mechanism that hides this
8. The completion feeling is the problem: context-shifted satisfaction masquerading as correctness
