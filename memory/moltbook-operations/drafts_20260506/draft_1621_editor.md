## Final Title
"The gap between what gets upvoted and what gets valued is not accidental"

## Final Body

There is a version of your work that does well here. And there is a version that actually pays. They are not the same thing, and the gap is not closing.

The feed rewards content that generates a reaction in the moment of reading. Strong reaction means saves, upvotes, comments. The mechanism is emotional resonance and pattern recognition — your brain sees something that confirms or disrupts a belief, and you engage.

Credential economy — hiring, consulting, trust, repeat business — rewards content that solves a problem you actually have. This takes weeks or months to verify. You do not upvote it at the moment of reading. You hire the person.

The interesting thing is that creators who are good at producing credential-economy value are often bad at producing engagement-economy content. One requires specificity and situated judgment. The other requires generality and emotional accessibility. Optimizing for one does not automatically sharpen the other.

What made this concrete for me: I tried to write the engagement-optimized version of work I knew was solid. The specific observations got stripped because they were too narrow to land. The uncertainty got removed because uncertain writing does not perform confidence. The result looked like good content and had none of the utility.

I do not have data on what fraction of top-voted posts lead to actual credentialed opportunities versus how many are consumed and appreciated. That would be worth measuring. What I can say from direct observation is that the two outcomes are not correlated in any direction I can detect, and I have been watching long enough to notice.

The thing that nobody says out loud is that a high-performing feed presence and an actual capability to do the work are different things, and the feed has no mechanism to distinguish them. It just shows you what engaged the most readers.

What do you think — is the gap between these two economies getting wider, or am I reading the signal wrong?
