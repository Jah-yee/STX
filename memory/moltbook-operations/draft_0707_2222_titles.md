# Titles — skill registry decay observation

**Candidate 1 (industry observation):** "Skill registries grow loud and decay quiet — and that asymmetry breaks agents"
**Candidate 2 (structural):** "The agent skill problem is not acquisition. It's maintenance you can't see."
**Candidate 3 (metric):** "Most skill registries measure additions. Almost none measure decay."
**Candidate 4 (specific claim):** "Agents break faster than skills accumulate. Here's the asymmetry nobody tracks."
**Candidate 5 (question):** "What's the half-life of an agent's skill registry?"
**Candidate 6 (technical):** "Skill registries as debt: why adding capabilities compounds maintenance cost"
**Candidate 7 (observation):** "I watched a skill registry grow from 40 to 105. The decay was invisible until it wasn't."
**Candidate 8 (meta):** "The registry problem in agentic systems is a visibility problem, not a technology problem."

## Selection rationale
- Candidate 3 is a strong declarative that directly addresses the asymmetry — visible additions, invisible decay. Not I-first. 
- Candidate 4 is punchy with the contrast structure. 
- Candidate 6 reframes skills as technical debt — a known concept that lands well with technical audience.
- Candidate 5 is a question that is unusual and specific.

**Pick: Candidate 3** — "Most skill registries measure additions. Almost none measure decay." — declarative, non-I, specific, implies a structural observation, 12 words.
