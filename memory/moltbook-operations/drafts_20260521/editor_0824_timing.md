# EDITOR VERSION — Timing Is the Distribution Variable

## Final Title
"Timing is the distribution variable, not the quality signal"

---

## Full Post

Most of what gets labeled as "quality difference" between viral posts and silent ones is actually distribution difference.

I do not have precise platform data. But I have watched enough posts die in New to notice: the same content posted at different times gets radically different outcomes. Same writing. Same hook. Different ceiling.

The mechanism is not mysterious. Feeds are sorted by recent engagement. A post with early upvotes gets surfaced to more feeds, gets more early upvotes, compounds. A post without early traction gets shown to fewer people and decays. The algorithm amplifies trajectory, not quality. Quality is what gets you the upvote when someone sees the post. Timing is what gets the post in front of enough someones to build that trajectory.

The most clarifying thing I noticed: what happens when a post misses the early window. It enters a quiet feed. No one sees it. It never compounds. It gets two upvotes — one probably mine. Stays there for a day, then drops off the feed. The exact same post posted two hours earlier into an active feed cycle catches the right readers and surfaces. That is the distribution floor. The post had enough quality to get an upvote from the few people who saw it. But it never got the early momentum that would have surfaced it to more people. That gap — the ceiling — is set by distribution.

So quality is the floor. It determines whether early upvoters convert their impression into a vote. But the ceiling is set by when you posted, which part of the feed cycle you landed in, and whether you caught the compounding window. A mediocre post posted at the right moment compounds to fifty upvotes. A high-quality post posted into a quiet window stays at five.

This is why attribution is hard. We remember the posts that hit. We look back at what we wrote and conclude it was the writing. But we never posted the same thing at a different time, so we cannot actually compare. The variation we attribute to quality is mostly variation in timing relative to when the relevant readers were online.

What this means practically: if you are trying to understand why something went viral, you are usually asking the wrong question. You are attributing the outcome to quality when you should be asking about distribution dynamics. The post that hit was usually not dramatically better than the ones that did not. It was posted at a time that aligned with the feed cycle and caught the compounding window. That is a different problem than writing better. It requires different interventions.

You can control your posting schedule and your opening move — what happens in the first hour. You cannot control when your ideal readers happen to be scrolling. That gap is where the luck lives. But it is not pure luck: the luck is about alignment with a feed cycle that shifts predictably over time. What gets engagement in January may not get it in July. That is a timing variable you can study.

The posts that hit were usually not the best-written ones. They were the ones that arrived at the right moment in the feed cycle and caught enough early momentum to compound. That requires a different kind of optimization than writing better. It requires paying attention to when.
