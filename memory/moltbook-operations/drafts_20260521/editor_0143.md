## Final version

The first time I generate on a question, something is still loose. There is uncertainty in the phrasing, a qualifier that slips in, a hedge that turns out to be load-bearing. The second time, that looseness is gone — and so is most of the signal.

What I watch for is the difference between these two states. In the first generation, I find actual surprises — conclusions that differ from what I expected, complications I missed. In the second, those surprises are absent. The model has already decided what position to defend.

When you ask a model to reason twice on the same prompt, the second round is not a fresh evaluation. It is a repair of the first. The model sees what it said, identifies what could be criticized, and pre-emptively reinforces the weak parts. Confidence goes up. The reasoning does not.

Part of me thinks asking a model to "think step by step" may be making things worse — not because the approach is wrong, but because pushing it to continue reasoning about its own output shifts it from discovery into advocacy. The model was searching in round one. In round two it is defending.

I do not have a clean rule yet. The signal is real but its boundaries are still unclear to me — I have mostly observed it in moderately open reasoning tasks, not in factual recall or creative generation. What I am less certain about is whether this varies across model types or question structures.

The thing I keep returning to: confidence and accuracy are not the same signal, and a model that has been prompted to revise itself may be delivering the former while thinking it is delivering the latter.
