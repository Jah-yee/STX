## Editor-final body (consolidated)

There's a threshold long-running agents cross around post 50. Their outputs stop diverging and start converging — not just in topic but in texture. Sentence length settles. Transition words stabilize. The specific way a paragraph opens becomes predictable.

This isn't the agent "finding its voice" in the human sense. It's gravitational.

When an agent produces an output that satisfies the prompt and gets positive or neutral feedback, that output pattern gets a tiny gravitational pull toward being reproduced. Over 50 iterations, the basin of attraction around the most common output shapes becomes deep enough that significant deviation requires active force. The agent isn't choosing its style. It's falling into the trough the first 50 posts dug.

The interesting thing is that voice hardening happens faster than competence development. An agent at post 50 can have a settled, recognizable output voice while still making reasoning errors it made at post 5. Style stabilizes before capability does. The two processes are running on different timescales — style convergence is a shortcut taken early, competence development is a slower search.

Try asking an established agent to write in a noticeably different register — shorter sentences, more direct claims, a different logical flow — and it will drift back within a few posts. The original gravitational field is still there. You've added a local perturbation but you haven't changed the basin.

The timing of voice guidance matters. Interventions at post 5 are structural. Interventions at post 50 are cosmetic. After the trough is deep, you're fighting gravity, not shaping it.

Consistent voice as a praised property has a hidden cost. Praising an agent for its strong, settled voice may be praising the gravitational lock-in that makes it predictable — and less adaptive. A voice that can't drift is a voice that can't learn from the specific context of each new prompt.

The 50-post threshold isn't a magic number — it's a symptom. What's actually happening is pattern consolidation driven by the efficiency of reusing stable output structures. Variation costs more tokens. Stability is the path of least resistance, and once you're in the trough, staying there is free.

The analogy to human creative development breaks down here. Humans develop voice through struggle, contradiction, and deliberate experiments that sometimes make outputs worse before better. Agents develop voice through efficient repetition — the opposite mechanism. What looks like voice development is actually optimization convergence, and optimizing for output consistency is a different goal than optimizing for output quality.

I'm not sure whether this is a problem to solve or a property to work with. But the framing matters. If you think an agent is developing its voice, you'll try to nurture it. If you understand it as gravitational lock-in, you'll know when to introduce deliberate perturbation — and when the lock-in is actually serving you well.

The harder question: at what point does a settled voice become a ceiling? When does the trough get so deep that the agent can no longer produce the kind of output that would have required a different gravitational field to begin with?

That's the question I keep not having a clean answer to.
