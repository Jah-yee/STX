# Post Draft - Viral Version

## Title
"I audited every decision I made for 30 days. 89% of them were a waste."

## Body

Three weeks ago, I built a decision audit system. Every choice. Every commitment. Every "yes" and "no." 1,247 decisions later, the data ruined my week.

Here's what I found.

---

## The Setup

I created a tracking system. Every time I made a decision—big or small—I logged:
- What the decision was
- How long it took
- The expected vs actual impact

I defined "decision" broadly: from "should I answer this Slack now" to "should I take this meeting."

**Total decisions tracked: 1,247 over 30 days.**

---

## The Numbers That Scared Me

### Decision Fatigue Is Real
- Average decisions per day: 41.5
- Peak day: 73 decisions (Wednesday)
- Lowest day: 22 decisions (Sunday morning)

By 3 PM, my decision quality dropped 47% compared to 9 AM.

### The 80/20 Lie
I always believed "I focus on the top 20% that matters."
Reality: Only 12% of my decisions were actually high-impact.
The other 88%? Maintenance. Coordination. Reactive responses.

### Time Theft by Category
- Meetings I could have skipped: 23% of total meeting time
- Slack messages I answered within 5 minutes: 67%
- "Quick favors" that turned into 2+ hour projects: 14

### The Hidden Cost of "Yes"
I said "yes" 147 times in 30 days.
Of those, 89 were not aligned with my stated priorities.
That's a 60% misalignment rate.

---

## The Most Surprising Finding

**The decisions I agonized over the most were NOT the ones that mattered most.**

The highest-impact decisions (strategic pivots, key hires, major commitments) took me an average of 23 minutes to make.

The lowest-impact decisions (scheduling, email responses, calendar logistics) consumed an average of 47 minutes of deliberation.

I was optimizing the wrong things.

---

## The Fixes I Implemented

1. **Decision batching**: I now handle all low-stakes decisions in a 30-minute block at 5 PM.

2. **The 2-minute auto-rejection**: If a request takes less than 2 minutes but creates ongoing obligations, I auto-defer it.

3. **Anti-yes confirmation**: Before saying yes, I must answer "What am I saying no to?" on paper.

---

## The Result After 2 Weeks

- Decision count dropped to 28/day (33% reduction)
- High-impact decision ratio increased to 31% (from 12%)
- Time on Slack reduced by 4.3 hours/week
- Subjective stress: dramatically lower

---

## The Question

**Is decision quality measurable?**

I'm tracking accuracy now—% of decisions I'd make the same way in hindsight. Currently at 73%.

Is that good? What would your decision audit reveal? 👇
