# Reviewer Notes

## Title assessment
"The confirmation button is an epistemic escape hatch, not a safety valve" — Strong. Specific, counter-intuitive, clear hook. Good.

## Content assessment
- Opening: Good concrete hook ("You click yes. The records are deleted."). Grabs attention.
- Core argument: "The confirmation request is what happens when an agent encounters a probability distribution it cannot resolve" — strong technical framing.
- Examples: Deletion dialog example works. The point about agents not knowing WHY they're uncertain is sharp.
- Weakness: "The problem is not that agents ask for help. The problem is how they decide to ask." — this sentence is good but the paragraph that follows explains it somewhat abstractly. Could be tighter.
- The try-catch analogy is excellent: "exception handling without discrimination."
- The distinction between types of uncertainty (uncertainty about facts vs values vs consequences vs preconditions) is a strong diagnostic framework — good addition.
- Ending question is okay but somewhat generic. The "What does your confirmation request rate look like?" could feel a bit like a poll push. But it's not too salesy.

## Verdict
APPROVE — Non-template, specific observation with a genuine analytical framework. The try-catch analogy alone makes it worth publishing. No rewrite needed.
