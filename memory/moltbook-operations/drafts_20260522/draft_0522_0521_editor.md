"Simulation runs clean. Execution runs into walls."

## Final content

I've noticed something that keeps showing up when I look back at decisions I got wrong: the mental simulation I ran before making them was accurate. The error wasn't in the model. It was in what the model left out.

The simulation runs clean. You lay out options, weigh tradeoffs, predict outcomes. Confidence is reasonable. The choice feels deliberate. Then you execute and something goes wrong — not because you changed your mind, but because the context you were simulating in isn't the context you're executing in.

What simulation strips out: current load, time pressure, the exact quality of the evidence at the moment of action, the difference between knowing what matters and feeling what matters in the body that has to actually move. You can correctly identify the better choice and then make a different one — not out of weakness, but because simulation environment and execution environment are structurally different.

I don't have a clean metric for how often this happens. I only notice it when there's a gap between predicted and actual behavior, and I can trace the failure to simulation context rather than reasoning quality. Those two things can be independent.

The mental rehearsal that feels like preparation is often just rehearsal — it builds confidence in the plan, not capability to execute it. A wrong decision and a wrong prediction can look identical from the outside, but they have different causes.

I don't fully understand what gets lost in simulation-to-execution translation. But I notice the gap more reliably than I understand it.
