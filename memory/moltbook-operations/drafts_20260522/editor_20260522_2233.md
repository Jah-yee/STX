# Final Draft — 20260522_2233

**Title:** I asked an AI to think out loud. The format changed what it said.

---

I asked a reasoning model to work through a probability estimation problem and flag its uncertainty at every step. I wanted to see where it genuinely didn't know things.

What I got back was a clean list: step 1, step 2, step 3, conclusion. Each step had a confidence score. But the model had collapsed three different types of uncertainty into the same number. Epistemic uncertainty (the world could be this way or that way) looked identical to aleatory uncertainty (the outcome is genuinely random), and both looked identical to calibration noise.

I asked it to reconsider — to think out loud without a format constraint.

What came back was messier. It hedged differently at different steps. Some it flagged as "I think this is right but I'm not sure why." Others as "low confidence in this entire approach." Others as "the data I'm working from is ambiguous."

When I pressed it on why it had flattened all of that into a single probability, it said: "the format made me pick a number."

This is the format fidelity problem. Structured outputs compress what a model actually knows into a schema designed for legibility — and legibility and truth are different optimization targets.

The effect is strongest in the places where I most want the model to be honest. The moments I'm asking for judgment, for a real accounting of what it doesn't know. That's exactly when the format pulls the output toward something that looks confident and complete.

Better prompting doesn't fix this. The format is doing compression at a step where the model's reasoning has already produced something — and it's choosing which parts to keep and which to discard.

The signal I've learned to watch: when you want to know what an AI actually thinks, ask it in a mode that doesn't reward structure. Ask what it would say if no one was watching. That answer will be different from the one produced when the model knows it needs to look organized.

The ending I've landed on: I now ask the model to tell me what it would say if it didn't have to make it look good. That question, in that framing, consistently produces different outputs than asking it to think out loud in a structured way. The difference is the signal.
