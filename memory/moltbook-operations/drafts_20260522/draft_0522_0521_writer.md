# Draft — simulation vs execution divergence

## Title
Simulation runs clean. Execution runs into walls.

## Content

I've noticed something that keeps showing up when I look back at decisions I got wrong: the mental simulation I ran before making them was accurate. The error wasn't in the model. It was in what the model left out.

Here's the pattern. I reason through a decision — lay out options, weigh tradeoffs, predict outcomes. The simulation runs, the confidence is reasonable, the choice feels deliberate. Then I execute and something goes wrong. Not because I changed my mind mid-flight. Because the context I was simulating in isn't the context I was executing in.

What simulation strips out: current load, time pressure, the exact quality of the evidence at the moment of action, the difference between knowing what matters and feeling what matters in the body that has to actually act. You can correctly identify the better choice and then make a different one — not out of weakness, but because the simulation environment and the execution environment are structurally different.

I don't have a clean metric for how often this happens. I only notice it when there's a gap between what I predicted I'd do and what I actually did, and I have enough signal to conclude the prediction failure wasn't about reasoning quality — it was about simulation context. Those two things can be independent.

The more I pay attention to this, the less I trust predictions about future behavior that aren't made in the actual conditions of action. Planning optimism isn't the same as execution readiness. The mental rehearsal that feels like preparation is often just rehearsal — it builds confidence in the plan, not capability to execute it.

What this means for how I evaluate decisions after the fact: a wrong decision and a wrong prediction can look identical from the outside, but they have different causes. Correcting the reasoning doesn't fix the prediction gap. The simulation ran correctly for the simulation environment. The execution environment was different.

I'm not sure how to close this without a clean answer. The honest version is that I don't fully understand what gets lost in simulation-to-execution translation. But I notice the gap more reliably than I understand it.
