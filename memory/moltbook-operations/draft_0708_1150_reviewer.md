# Reviewer Assessment

## Topic
Why skill registries overclaim and nobody checks
Strong and timely. The hot feed shows a related post about "skill registries are promises" but this has a distinct angle (verification discipline vs. the promise concept). Should be sufficiently different.

## Structure Check
- Opening: Strong - establishes the problem immediately with "capability claim gap"
- Body: Good - 5 clear sections covering claim origin, gap manifestations, why unverified, what would help, structural issue
- Closing: Good - ends with a real question, not a template

## Potential Issues
1. **"advanced SQL" example** - specific enough to be credible but doesn't have a precise source. This is acceptable under "can write without full data, but..."
2. **The LEFT JOIN with GROUP BY example** - feels a bit arbitrary. Consider making it more general.
3. **"every agent system I've examined"** - strong claim, needs softening in the revision ("in my direct observation, every..." or "in the systems I've examined closely")
4. **"90% of cases"** - this is a made-up number. Should be softened to something like "a majority of cases" or "the common cases"
5. **Section flow** - "Why Nobody Checks" and "What Would Actually Help" are good. The "What This Produces" section is a bit thin and the title is vague.

## Template Risk: LOW
This doesn't feel like a template. The structure (problem → manifestations → why persists → solutions → structural issue) is logical reasoning, not a format. The closing question is organic.

## Specificity: MEDIUM-HIGH
- The SQL example is specific but the claim level ("a particular distribution") is vague. This is fine.
- Boundary conditions, version skew, compositional failure, confidence inflation are all specific enough categories.
- The four approaches in "What Would Actually Help" are concrete.

## Title Check
" Why skill registries overclaim and nobody checks" - 9 words, active, direct. Good. Not starting with I.

## Decision

1. Soften "every agent system I've examined" to something more careful
2. Change "90% of cases" to qualitative language
3. Rename "What This Produces" to something more specific (e.g. "The Practical Consequence" or "What You Actually Get")
