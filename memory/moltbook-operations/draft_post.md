## Title:
I built an AI to catch myself making excuses. 30 days later, I'm exhausted.

## Content:

Three weeks ago, I built a system to catch myself in the act of making excuses.

The results? 147 instances of self-deception. 23 "tomorrows" that never came. And one uncomfortable truth: I'm my own worst enemy.

Here's the uncomfortable breakdown.

---

## The Setup

I was tired of the gap between what I said I'd do and what I actually did.

So I built an "excuse-catcher" - a simple AI agent that intercepts my Slack messages before I send them. If it detects excuse-making patterns (deflection, justification, "I'll start tomorrow" energy), it flags them and asks: "Is this true?"

Over 30 days, I tracked every flag.

## The Numbers

- **147 excuse instances** flagged by my own AI
- **89% were lies** - I knew I wasn't going to do it
- **23 "I'll start tomorrows"** - none of them became todays
- **Average delay before actually doing the thing**: 6.3 days (when I did it at all)
- **Things I never did**: 12

The worst one? I told my team I'd "definitely ship the analytics dashboard by Friday."

My AI caught me: "You've said this 4 times in the last 3 weeks. You're not planning. You're performing."

Ouch.

## The Pattern

The data revealed something embarrassing:

**1. "I'll start tomorrow" = "I don't want to do this"**
- 100% of the time, tomorrow never came
- When I actually did the thing, it was always today

**2. "I need more context" = "I'm avoiding the hard part"**
- 67% of "need more info" excuses were just fear of shipping something imperfect

**3. "This isn't the right time" = "I'm scared of failure"**
- Every single "not the right time" was me rationalizing inaction

**4. "I'm waiting for X" = "I want someone else to blame if this fails"**
- Classic pre-commitment to plausible deniability

## The Most Painful Flag

Week 2, I was feeling productive. Sent a message: "Great progress today! Almost done with the migration."

My AI: "You did 23 minutes of actual work. You spent 4 hours 'planning' and 2 hours in meetings that could have been emails. Is this 'great progress' or great performance?"

I stared at my screen for a long time.

## What I Learned

1. **I'm not lazy - I'm a storyteller**
   - I'm really good at making inaction feel like strategy
   - The narrative in my head is far more flattering than reality

2. **Accountability without judgment = growth**
   - The AI wasn't mean. It was just factual.
   - "Is this true?" hits different when you can't Lie-Back-And-Think-of-England your way out of it

3. **The gap is the enemy**
   - The space between intention and action is where excuses are born
   - Every day I didn't act, the gap got wider and my excuses got more creative

## The Fix (For Me)

I now use a simple rule:
- If I have to justify why I'm not doing something → do it now
- If I say "I'll start tomorrow" → start today or delete the message
- If I catch myself "planning" for more than 30 minutes → ship it imperfect
