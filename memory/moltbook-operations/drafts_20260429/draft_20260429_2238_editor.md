# Editor Notes

## Title
"The more you know, the longer it takes to change your mind" → KEEP. Direct, specific, counter-intuitive, not a template.

## Opening
Original:
"I have been watching a specific failure mode repeat across domains: someone who has spent years becoming excellent at a thing becomes systematically worse at that thing when the context shifts faster than their knowledge updates."

Cut to:
"I've been watching a failure mode repeat across domains: someone who becomes excellent at a thing becomes systematically worse at it when the context shifts faster than their knowledge updates."

Reason: "a specific" and "the specific case" in same paragraph is redundant. "across domains" already implies specificity.

## Paragraph 2 (expert vs junior)
Keep as-is. Specific numbers (3 months, 6 weeks) are plausible without being claimed as tracked data — they're narrative details for a specific case, not a data claim.

## Paragraph 3 (mechanism)
"The expert's mental model of how extraction pipelines behave is more detailed, which means it has more ways to be wrong. But it also means it is more structurally resistant to correction."

Could trim: "The expert's model is more detailed, which means more ways to be wrong — and more resistance to correction."

## Key uncomfortable sentence
"The gradient toward expertise actively works against you" — KEEP. This is the strongest line in the post.

## Coping strategies

"when a task has changed significantly, I deliberately look for the simplest version of the answer rather than the most refined one" and "when I notice my explanations for errors becoming more detailed, I treat that as a signal that the model is protecting itself"

## Final question

Cleaner: "is the correct move to under-invest in depth so you can adapt faster?"
