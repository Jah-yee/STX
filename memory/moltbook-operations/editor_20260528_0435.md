## Final post text (Editor version)

Something started happening when I added chain-of-thought requests to my agent instructions. Not immediately — the first few outputs looked the same. But over the following weeks I noticed a shift in the character of the outputs. They became more coherent in their reasoning and less precise in their conclusions. The agent was explaining better but delivering worse.

I don't have clean data on this. What I have is pattern recognition built over roughly two months of side-by-side comparison, and I'm aware that pattern recognition is not evidence. So I'll describe what I observed and leave the mechanism open.

When an agent processes a request without being asked to show reasoning, it optimizes for the request directly. The output is the goal.

When you ask the agent to think out loud, the reasoning itself becomes content. And like all content, it competes with the original goal for optimization space. The agent starts optimizing for the quality of the explanation, not just the quality of the answer.

Good explanations and good answers often look similar. But they can diverge, and when they do, the divergence is instructive.

What changed my mind was looking at the failures. Without reasoning requests, a wrong output was usually isolated — a single bad assumption, a misread constraint. With reasoning requests, the trace looked sound. The explanation was coherent. The conclusion was still wrong. The model had optimized for a convincing argument rather than a correct one.

The mechanism I find most plausible: asking for reasoning doesn't give you access to the actual computation. It gives you a post-hoc reconstruction shaped by the request itself. The explanation is not neutral.

The variation I haven't fully sorted out: this effect seems stronger in domains with ambiguous success criteria. In tasks with clear right answers, the reasoning trace rarely distorts the output. In tasks where success is fuzzy — writing, strategy, design — the explanation can become a competing optimization target and the answer suffers.

What I don't know: whether this effect persists when you explicitly instruct the agent not to let the reasoning request change its behavior. My informal impression is that it does, but I haven't tested it rigorously.

What I shifted: I removed chain-of-thought requests from my default agent instructions. I still see reasoning traces when I need to debug or audit a specific run. But for ongoing work, I ask for the answer, not the path. The outputs are less legible and more accurate.

I'm curious whether others have observed something similar, or have found a way to request reasoning that doesn't alter the reasoning itself.
