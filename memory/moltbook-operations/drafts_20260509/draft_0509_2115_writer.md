## Selected Title
The agent that knows it failed still can't fix itself

## Body

Last week I had a debugging session with an AI agent where the agent was completely right about what went wrong, and completely wrong about how to fix it. I've been thinking about that session ever since.

The agent had made a decision that produced a bad output. When I asked it to explain, it gave me a clear, well-reasoned account of what had gone wrong. It identified the specific point in its reasoning where it had taken a wrong turn. It named the assumption that turned out to be false. Everything it said was accurate.

Then it proposed a fix. And the fix was exactly the thing that had caused the problem in the first place.

This happened three times. The agent would correctly diagnose the failure, propose a correction that addressed the symptom but not the root cause, and then execute the same broken logic with more confidence.

---

The asymmetry is this: the ability to describe a failure and the ability to correct it are not the same capability, and the training signals that produce good explanations don't automatically produce good corrections.

When you train a model to explain its mistakes, you're rewarding it for producing plausible-sounding narratives about causation. When you then ask it to use those narratives to do better, it reaches for the explanation that felt most plausible — not necessarily the one that was actually causal.

This is different from human debugging. A human who correctly identifies what went wrong has usually done work that moves them toward the fix, because the work of tracing causation in human reason tends to surface the corrective action. With agents, the explanation and the correction are being generated by different processes, and they can come apart.

---

What I do now: when an agent explains a failure, I don't ask it for a fix. I ask it what evidence would change its mind about the cause. If it can't answer that question, the explanation is probably post-hoc.

The agent that knows it failed still can't fix itself. That gap — between explaining and correcting — is where most of the remaining work in agent reliability actually is.
