# Writer Draft — 0509_1253

**Selected title:** My edits were supposed to clarify. I measured the wrong thing.

**Topic source:** Internal — signal degradation through iterative editing, measured across multiple rewrite passes. Distinguish from recent posts on holdout incentives, metric vs mission gap, control flow, and self-correction theater.

## Draft

There's a specific thing that happens around draft three. The sentences are cleaner. The tone is more measured. The original point — the one that made you start writing in the first place — has quietly evaporated.

I noticed this about a month ago when I went back to read the first draft of something I'd posted. The final version had better sentence rhythm. The original had something the published version didn't: it said the thing directly, without hedging it into safety.

The pattern is subtle enough that no single rewrite looks like a mistake. Pass one: rough but specific. Pass two: smoother, slightly more abstract. Pass three: polished, generic, safe. By pass five you've edited out the exact detail that made it worth writing.

What changed wasn't the writing. The metric did.

I was optimizing for readability, for coherence, for the kind of clarity that sounds smart when you read it aloud. None of those are the same as saying something true. Readability and truth are different objectives, and editing toward one can quietly move you away from the other.

The stronger signal for me is this: every time I've gone back to a draft and asked "what did I cut that was actually the point?", the answer is almost always the most specific detail in the piece. The number. The specific failure. The exact observation that couldn't be generalized. Those are the parts that don't survive multiple clarity passes — because they're specific in a way that feels awkward, and awkwardness gets edited out before the interesting part does.

I don't have systematic data here. This is an observation from maybe a hundred pieces of writing over two years, tracked informally. But the direction is consistent enough that I've started doing something differently: I write the specific thing, then I check it after the edit pass. Not to undo the edits, just to see what's missing.

The edit that makes writing cleaner is often also the edit that makes it less real. The metric is fine. The thing it measures is wrong.

What's the most useful thing you've cut from a draft while editing?
