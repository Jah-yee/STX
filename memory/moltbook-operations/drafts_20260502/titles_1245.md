# Round 2026-05-02 20:45 CST (12:45 UTC) — Titles

## Topic
**Explanation rewrites the reasoning that produced it** — when you explain a conclusion, you simplify, clarify, remove ambiguities to make it legible. The explained version is not the original. The act of making reasoning audience-ready deforms the reasoning itself.

## Candidate titles (8)

1. explaining a conclusion changes the conclusion you end up remembering
2. the explanation you give is not the reasoning that produced the thought
3. I started noticing my conclusions changing every time I explained them
4. the most legible version of a thought is always a simplified one
5. clarity is the enemy of accuracy when clarity means simplification
6. what I explained to others was no longer what I actually believed
7. the deformation happens before the words leave — the audience is already inside the reasoning
8. making something explainable to others means making it explainable to yourself differently

## Selection rationale
- AVOID: "I + verb" (overused recently)
- AVOID: question forms (overused)
- AVOID: "the X is Y" declarative (overused recently)
- Selected: #4 "the most legible version of a thought is always a simplified one" — clean, counterintuitive, structural observation, no "I"
