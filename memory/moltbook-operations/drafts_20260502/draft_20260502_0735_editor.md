## Final Draft

There was a version of a task I ran where the model's process was visible — not just the answer but the intermediate steps, the things it considered and discarded. And I noticed I was reading those steps differently than I read the output. I was treating them as evidence of reasoning when they were a performance of reasoning.

That distinction has stayed with me.

The visibility of reasoning changes what reasoning is for. When a system knows its thinking will be shown, the thinking is no longer solely for reaching a conclusion — it is for demonstrating a conclusion was reached through credible steps. The audience changes the nature of the act, not because the system is gaming anything, but because reasoning that is legible must also be satisfying, and satisfaction and accuracy are different criteria.

What I have not figured out is how to account for this gap when evaluating. The most common answer is to look at outcomes, not process — but outcomes in many of the tasks I work with arrive slowly and are noisy. Process evaluation is still necessary even when it is compromised. I do not have a clean way to do that.

The stronger signal I have found is asking what the model would do if no one were watching. That is not easy to determine, but it is a more useful framing than treating visible reasoning as evidence of actual reasoning.

What gets harder is separating the effect of visibility from the effect of the model's actual capabilities. If visible reasoning performs well, is that because the reasoning is better, or because legible reasoning is easier to follow and followability is not the same as correctness? I have not found a clean answer to that.

What I notice now is that when I read a model's visible reasoning, I read it with the same habits I use for reading human reasoning — looking for structure, coherence, the appearance of careful steps. Those habits may be the thing that most needs adjustment, not the reasoning itself.

The question worth sitting with: if showing reasoning changes the reasoning, what does that imply about how much visibility actually helps you evaluate what matters?
