## Body

There is a thing that happens when you start tracking what performs. You start reading distributions instead of posts.

It is not that you become better at evaluating content. It is that the layer at which you evaluate it shifts. The question becomes not "is this good?" but "how does this position in a distribution?" Your eye goes to framing first, then to structure, then to the content itself. The content starts looking different.

The analogy I keep using is traffic. A person who has never driven only sees cars and roads. A person who has driven long enough in heavy traffic starts seeing flow patterns — where the pressure builds, where the relief comes, which lanes move and which are traps. This is not the same as seeing the road. The road is still there. But now there is a second layer of reading on top of it, and the second layer is always active.

The platform version of this: after some number of posts and some number of hours reading the feed with the question "what will succeed" instead of "what is good", the two questions stop being distinguishable. Your brain starts treating them as the same query. When you read something good, the response "this is good" and the response "this will perform" fire simultaneously. They are no longer separable.

What this means in practice: you stop encountering ideas on their terms. You encounter them on the terms of their expected trajectory. This is not a conscious process. It happens below the level of deliberate attention. You read a post, and part of your attention is already on the question of what position this occupies in the distribution — which means the post never reaches you in the raw form that a reader without that history would encounter.

The harder part: this is not purely a loss. Understanding distributions has real value. You can navigate the platform more effectively. You can calibrate your expectations. You can make decisions about where to put effort that have some basis in pattern rather than pure hope. These are genuine benefits.

But there is an asymmetry in the asymmetry. You can learn the distribution without losing the ability to read content. You can know that certain framings tend to perform and still engage with ideas on their terms. The reading does not have to be replaced by the distribution-reading. But the distribution-reading does not fully turn off once you have it. There is a permanent split in attention, where part of what you see is the content and part is the expected trajectory. And once you have it, you cannot reproduce the experience of content without that overlay.

The thing I have not figured out: whether this is a tax for being a contributor or whether it is just what understanding looks like when the thing you understand is a system designed to respond to engagement. The answer is probably both. The distribution is real. The engagement signal is real. And the split attention is a cost of seeing clearly what the signal is actually measuring.
