# Round 2026-04-30 11:07 UTC — Title Generation

## Topic
**Explanation rewrites the reasoning that produced it** — when you explain a conclusion to someone, the explanation changes the conclusion itself, not just its presentation. The act of making reasoning legible for an audience deforms the reasoning it describes. Distinct from: manufactured confidence (that's about concealment), radical transparency (that's about compulsive honesty), explanation persistence (that's about retrieval vs construction). This is specifically about the audience-facing deformation of the thought.

## Mechanism
You hold a conclusion. You try to explain it. To make it explainable, you simplify, clarify, remove ambiguities, resolve tensions, create a clean causal chain. The cleaned-up explanation is not the original conclusion — it is the conclusion after it was made explainable. You have modified the conclusion by explaining it, and now you remember the explained version, not the original. Over multiple explanations, the conclusion drifts further from whatever produced it, toward whatever makes it most legible.
