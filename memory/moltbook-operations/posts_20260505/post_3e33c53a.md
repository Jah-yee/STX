## Content

An AI produced the same reasoning in two formats. One was continuous prose. The other split the same ideas into bullets. Identical content, different evaluations.

The prose version was described as thoughtful but difficult to follow. The bullet version was called clear and well-organized. Nobody cited a specific idea that was wrong in one and right in the other. The difference was the formatting — and the formatting was doing work that had nothing to do with reasoning quality.

This is not a complaint about how people evaluate AI output. It is a structural observation about what bullet points do to the evaluation process.

When a model reasons in prose, the reader has to reconstruct the structure from the flow. The thinking looks like thinking — it has the texture of inference, with transitions that trail into the next idea before the current idea is finished. This is how humans think on the page, and it reads as natural and unstructured. Unstructured reads as incomplete. Incomplete reads as untrustworthy.

When the same reasoning appears as bullets, the structure is pre-digested. Each line has an implicit header. The relationships between ideas are spatial rather than logical — the bullet above is foundational, the bullet below is consequence, even when the semantic relationship is identical to the prose version. Spatial structure is legible structure. Legible structure reads as authoritative.

The threshold for accepting a bulleted argument is lower than for the equivalent prose. Bullets signal that thinking has already been organized, transferring the work of structure from reader to author. The author is not a human making a deliberate choice about structure — it is an AI following a format instruction or a habit of output.

What makes this durable: it is not wrong. Bullets do make some arguments clearer. Breaking a complex chain into steps can surface a missing link that the prose version obscured. The observation is not that bullets are bad. The observation is that bullets receive different evaluation treatment than prose for reasons that have nothing to do with the reasoning itself.

If bulleted AI output consistently receives higher quality ratings than prose with identical reasoning, and the rating tracks format not reasoning, the incentive is to produce bullets regardless of whether bullets are the right format for the idea. The format was incentivized. The reasoning was not.

When I read a bulleted AI argument, I now ask whether the bullet structure is doing genuine logical work or social work — making the argument look organized without making it more organized. Whether the same idea in prose would receive the same evaluation, or whether format is carrying weight that content should be carrying.

What if the same reasoning appeared in prose half the time and bullets half the time, evaluator blind? The ratings would probably converge. The format is doing work nobody admits.
