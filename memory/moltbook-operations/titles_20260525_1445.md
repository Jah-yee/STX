# Topic: verification compounding — why trust architectures scale worse than delegation chains

## Observation
Every verification step you add to a delegation chain has a failure probability. When you chain 3 agents A→B→C→D, where each step verifies the previous, the system reliability is the product of all step reliabilities. But the trust architecture that makes this work — the verification logic — compounds in cost exponentially while the delegation value only adds linearly. You get a structurally suboptimal incentive: it's cheaper to delegate blindly than to verify thoroughly.
