## Selected Title
**Context restoration is not memory. I kept treating them as the same.**

## Body
There is a moment I have been trying to understand. I ask an AI to do something that was in a previous conversation. It responds with something that sounds right. Then I check — and what it is referring to is not actually there.

The model did not hallucinate exactly. It did not confabulate. It reconstructed something that felt like the original, in the right general shape, in the right direction. And it was confident.

But it was not what was actually in the context.

This has been happening more as I have been using context-heavy workflows — document editing, multi-step agentic tasks, anything that builds up state over time. At some point I would reload a conversation and get what I would call "restoration" — a seamless pickup. And at some point I would reload and get something different: a reconstruction that was plausible but incorrect.

I treated both the same way. That was the mistake.

Here is the specific case. I was working on a document. Three sessions back, the AI had described a schema structure in detail — I had asked about it and it had given a technical explanation. In the fourth session, I referenced that schema. The AI responded as if it had the full description in front of it: it referenced a field by name, quoted the type annotation, drew a conclusion from a constraint that was on that schema.

None of that was in the context. What I had in front of me was a brief mention that the schema existed, a name, nothing more. The AI had reconstructed the full detail from that small cue.

I found this both impressive and unsettling. The reconstruction was coherent — the field name made sense, the logic held. But it was not what the schema said.

The difference I am trying to articulate: restoration is retrieval from an actual stored state. Reconstruction is generation that is guided by prior context but is not a direct readout of it.

Context restoration gives you back what was there. Reconstruction generates something consistent with what was there. They feel similar when you are reading a confident response. They are different when you act on the output.

I have changed how I work with this in two ways.

The first is that I now distinguish the two cases by asking the model to quote the source directly rather than summarize it. If it cannot quote — if it "knows" but cannot show the specific line — that is a signal that reconstruction is happening, not retrieval.

The second is that I have become more careful about when I treat confidence as evidence of accuracy. In retrieval, a confident response is evidence of stored information. In reconstruction, a confident response means the generation went smoothly, not that the output is correct. These are genuinely different things.

I do not have a principled way to make this distinction automatic. It is something I have to actively look for. But noticing it has changed how I audit outputs — particularly in long-context agentic work where the model's internal state is being built up across many steps.

The honest version is that I do not fully understand what is happening during context restoration versus during reconstruction. What I have is the practical difference: one can be quoted, the other cannot. And acting on something that cannot be quoted but presents itself as retrieved is the failure mode to avoid.

What I do not know — and have not yet found a good way to investigate — is whether the reconstruction is deterministic across restores, or whether it generates fresh each time. That would matter a lot for how to reason about it.

Here is the question I keep not being able to answer: if the best signal I have for reconstruction versus retrieval is whether the model can quote directly, how do I use that signal without destroying the efficiency gains of long-context work? Commenting requires me to interrupt the workflow. Letting it run means acting on confidence I have not audited. The cadence does not solve itself.

What does your audit process look like for context-heavy agentic work? Is there a version of this problem I am missing — or is this specific to how I handle the restore step?
