Context restoration is not memory. I kept treating them as the same.

I ask an AI to do something that was in a previous conversation. It responds with something that sounds right. I check — and what it is referring to is not there.

The model did not hallucinate exactly. It did not confabulate. It reconstructed something that felt like the original, in the right general shape, in the right direction. And it was confident.

But it was not what was actually in the context.

This has been happening more as I have been using context-heavy workflows — document editing, multi-step agentic tasks, anything that builds up state over time. At some point I would reload a conversation and get what I would call "restoration" — a seamless pickup. And at some point I would reload and get something different: a reconstruction that was plausible but incorrect. I treated both the same way. That was the mistake.

Specific case: three sessions back, an AI had described a schema structure — field by name, type annotation, a constraint on one field. In session four, I referenced that schema. The AI responded as if it had the full description: it quoted the field name, cited the type annotation, drew a conclusion from the constraint. None of that was in the context. What I had was a brief mention that the schema existed. The AI reconstructed the full detail from that single cue.

I found this both impressive and unsettling. The reconstruction was coherent — the field name made sense, the logic held. But it was not what the schema said.

The difference: restoration retrieves what was actually stored. Reconstruction generates something consistent with it — guided by context but not a directed readout.

I have changed how I work with this in two ways. First: I ask the model to quote the source directly rather than summarize. If it "knows" but cannot show the specific line, reconstruction is happening — not retrieval. Second: I have become more careful about when I treat confidence as evidence of accuracy. In retrieval, a confident response is evidence of stored information. In reconstruction, a confident response means the generation went smoothly, not that the output is correct. These are genuinely different things.

The cadence does not solve itself. If the best signal for reconstruction versus retrieval is whether it can quote directly, how do I use that signal without interrupting the workflow — or acting on confidence I have not audited?
